The Deccan

Hyderabadi Cuisine

Persian court cooking met Telugu spice tradition here. Four centuries in the making, with one dish at the center of it all.

Two dynasties, one fusion cuisine

Hyderabadi cuisine is a fusion in the most literal sense. Its history is unusually well documented because it grew up as a court cuisine, shaped deliberately by ruling dynasties rather than by agriculture and geography alone. The Qutb Shahi dynasty, which ruled the Golconda Sultanate from the 16th to 17th century, brought Persian court cooking to the Deccan. When the Mughal Empire later absorbed the region and the Nizams (the Asaf Jahi dynasty) took power in the 18th century, their kitchens combined that Persian-Mughal foundation with the spice-forward Telugu and Marathwada cooking already established locally. The result is rich and fragrant on its Persian-Mughal side and sharply spiced on its Deccan side.

The dum technique

What sets Hyderabadi biryani apart from other Indian biryanis is the cooking method rather than the spice blend. In the "kachi" (raw) style, marinated raw meat is layered directly with partially cooked rice. The pot is sealed and slow-cooked over low heat (dum), so the rice finishes cooking in the steam and juices the meat releases. The two are never cooked separately and combined afterward. This layering-and-sealing method, often done by sealing the lid with dough to trap steam, came from Persian court kitchens. Its survival in Hyderabad is a direct trace of the Nizams' Persian-influenced cooks.

The layering-and-sealing technique behind Hyderabadi biryani is a 300-year-old Persian court method, still practiced almost exactly as it was.

Haleem and the Nizami table

Haleem, a dense, slow-pounded stew of wheat, lentils, and meat, has similarly deep roots. It traces back to Arab and Persian meat-and-wheat porridges (related to the Middle Eastern harees), brought into the Nizams' kitchens and adapted with local spice. In Hyderabad it is now tied closely to Ramadan, served as an iftar dish across the city each evening during the fasting month.

Quick facts

Region
Deccan Plateau (Hyderabad, Telangana)
Staple grain
Rice
Defining technique
Dum: sealed-pot, layered slow-cooking
Key historical influence
Qutb Shahi & Nizami (Asaf Jahi) court kitchens, 16th to 20th century
Signature dish
Hyderabadi biryani

Signature dishes

  • Hyderabadi biryani: layered, dum-cooked rice and marinated meat.
  • Haleem: a slow-pounded wheat, lentil, and meat stew, a Ramadan specialty.
  • Mirchi ka salan: a tangy chili curry in a peanut-sesame gravy.
  • Double ka meetha: a bread pudding fried and soaked in sweetened milk, of Persian dessert lineage.
  • Qubani ka meetha: a stewed apricot dessert, served with cream.
