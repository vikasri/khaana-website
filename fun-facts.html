Fun facts

Food Trivia

Four questions a day, drawn from 60. They change at midnight and the whole set comes round every 15 days, so there is no way to binge it and no reason to come back twice in one day.

  1. 1Indian food is famous for chilli heat. When did the chilli actually reach India?

    Chillies are American. The Portuguese brought them to Goa in the 1500s and they spread across the subcontinent in roughly a century. Every fiery curry you can think of is, historically speaking, a recent development.

  2. 2So what made Indian food hot before chillies turned up?

    Pepper did the work, which is why it was worth more than gold to medieval Europe. Long pepper (pippali) was the sharper of the two and has largely fallen out of use.

  3. 3Where would you find a jar of 'curry powder' in India?

    It was blended for colonial officers who wanted one tin to reproduce a whole cuisine. Indian cooks mix spices per dish, and often per stage of the same dish.

  4. 4Vindaloo gets its name from...

    It is a Portuguese marinade of wine and garlic that landed in Goa and met palm vinegar and chillies. The 'aloo' is a coincidence. Putting potatoes in it is a misunderstanding with a long and happy career.

  5. 5The samosa arrived in India from where?

    It travelled as sanbusak along trade routes and turns up in Persian writing well before it reached Delhi. The potato filling had to wait for the Portuguese to bring potatoes.

  6. 6Which of these is actually native to India?

    Pepper is from the Malabar coast. Potato, tomato and chilli are all American, and all three arrived in the last five hundred years.

  7. 7Roughly how many crocus flowers does it take to make one gram of saffron?

    Each flower gives three red stigmas, picked by hand at dawn. That is the whole reason saffron costs what it does — there is no way to mechanise it.

  8. 8The bhut jolokia, or ghost pepper, comes from which part of India?

    It grows in Assam, Nagaland and Manipur, and in 2007 Guinness certified it as the world's hottest chilli. It has since been comfortably overtaken, which tells you something about the people breeding chillies.

  9. 9Why does milk help with chilli burn when water does not?

    Capsaicin is oil-soluble, so water just moves it around. Casein, a protein in dairy, lifts it off the pain receptors. Lassi with a hot meal is not decoration.

  10. 10Which part of a chilli is hottest?

    The placenta, that white rib inside, is where capsaicin is made. Seeds only taste hot because they have been sitting against it. Deseeding a chilli works mostly because you scrape the pith out along with them.

  11. 11About what share of the world's turmeric does India grow?

    India grows the great majority of it and also eats the great majority of it, so relatively little enters world trade.

  12. 12Hing, the asafoetida in your tadka, is mostly...

    The plant barely grows in India. A country that puts hing in a great deal of its cooking imports almost every gram of it, and has done for centuries.

  13. 13Nutmeg and mace are...

    Mace is the scarlet lacy covering wrapped around the nutmeg seed. One fruit, two spices, two prices.

  14. 14Tandoori chicken as we know it was popularised at which Delhi restaurant?

    Kundan Lal Gujral brought it from Peshawar after Partition. Butter chicken came out of the same kitchen, invented to rescue tandoori chicken that had dried out.

  15. 15Butter chicken was invented to solve what problem?

    Unsold tandoori chicken went into a tomato, butter and cream gravy to bring it back. One of the world's most ordered dishes began as waste management.

  16. 16Chicken tikka masala is most often credited to a kitchen in...

    The best-known story has a Glasgow cook improvising a sauce for a customer who complained his chicken was dry. The claim is disputed, which has never slowed the dish down.

  17. 17Tomato ketchup descends from what?

    The word comes from kê-tsiap, a fermented fish brine from southern China and Southeast Asia. Tomatoes were a late substitution, and the fish quietly dropped out.

  18. 18What does the 'jamun' in gulab jamun refer to?

    Gulab is rose water, jamun is the java plum, which the fried balls resemble in size and colour. The dish is named for looking like fruit it is not.

  19. 19Why does paneer hold its shape in a hot curry instead of melting?

    Curdling milk with lemon or vinegar sets the protein in a way that will not flow again with heat. Rennet cheeses like mozzarella melt; acid-set ones like paneer and halloumi grill instead.

  20. 20What makes idli batter rise overnight?

    Nothing is added. Microbes already on the grain and on the cook's hands do it, which is why the same recipe behaves differently in different kitchens and different seasons.

  21. 21Garam masala literally means 'hot spice'. Hot in what sense?

    It is the Ayurvedic sense of warming, not pungent. A classic garam masala often contains no chilli at all — cinnamon, cardamom, clove, pepper, cumin.

  22. 22Basmati means...

    From Sanskrit roots meaning aroma. The name is about the smell, not the shape, which is the right way round: the smell is the point.

  23. 23The usual distinction between biryani and pulao is that...

    In a pulao the rice cooks in the stock and absorbs it. In a biryani the two are cooked apart and layered, then finished sealed. Plenty of families will argue with this, at length.

  24. 24In dum cooking, what traditionally seals the pot?

    A rope of atta dough pressed around the rim traps the steam. It bakes hard during cooking and gets broken open at the table, which is half the theatre.

  25. 25What makes kulfi denser than ice cream?

    Ice cream is up to half air by volume. Kulfi is frozen still, in sealed moulds, so it stays solid and melts far more slowly.

  26. 26Why do Indians wince slightly at the phrase 'chai tea'?

    Chai is tea in Hindi and a dozen other languages, from the same Chinese root as the English word. The drink people mean is masala chai.

  27. 27South Indian filter coffee traditionally contains what besides coffee?

    Usually 10 to 20 per cent chicory root, which arrived as a wartime extender and stayed because people preferred the taste. It is what gives the decoction its body.

  28. 28Before the British planted tea in Assam, what did India mostly do with tea?

    The tea plant grew wild in Assam but was not a national drink. Mass tea drinking was manufactured by a marketing campaign in the early 1900s, and it worked spectacularly.

  29. 29Which country produces the most milk?

    India has led since the 1990s, on the back of a cooperative movement that turned millions of one and two-animal households into a national dairy supply.

  30. 30Which country grows the most mangoes?

    India grows around half the world's mangoes and exports very few of them, because they get eaten at home. There are well over a thousand named varieties.

  31. 31Which is the most expensive spice by weight?

    Saffron, by a distance. Vanilla is usually second, and both are expensive for the same reason: the harvest cannot be mechanised.

  32. 32Black pepper was once called 'black gold'. Which Indian coast built its wealth on it?

    The Malabar coast of Kerala. The search for a sea route to that pepper is the reason Vasco da Gama sailed, and the reason a great deal of subsequent history happened.

  33. 33Jalebi's name comes from...

    It arrived with Persian and Arab traders as zulabiya. Nearly every sweet-shop favourite has a passport, which is rather the point of Indian food.

  34. 34A papad puffs and blisters over a flame because...

    The dough is dried, not dead: the little moisture left flashes to steam and lifts the layers. The same physics puffs a puri and a phulka.

  35. 35For a puri to balloon properly, the oil must be...

    Hot oil sets the outside instantly, so the steam inside has nowhere to go but up. Cool oil lets it soak in and you get a heavy, greasy disc.

  36. 36Jaggery, or gur, is...

    Cane juice boiled down without separating out the molasses, which is where the colour and the mineral taste come from. Palm and date versions are common too.

  37. 37The English word 'sugar' traces back to which language?

    Sanskrit sharkara, through Arabic and Italian into English. Crystallising sugar from cane was worked out in India, and the word travelled with the technique.

  38. 38In Sanskrit the coconut palm is kalpavriksha. What does that mean?

    Rather generous, but defensible: the fruit, the water, the oil, the milk, the shell, the fibre, the leaves and the timber all get used.

  39. 39Eating off a banana leaf is not only tradition. What does the leaf do?

    Hot food draws a faint aroma and some polyphenols out of the wax. It is also a plate nobody has to wash, which may be the more persuasive argument.

  40. 40West Bengal and Odisha both hold a GI tag connected to rasgulla. What settled it?

    Bengal was granted one for Banglar Rasogolla in 2017 and Odisha one for Odisha Rasagola in 2019. Two tags, two sweets, and an argument that carries on regardless.

  41. 41The fortune cookie was invented in...

    It descends from a Japanese cracker and was popularised in California. It is served in Chinese restaurants nearly everywhere except China.

  42. 42Bhindi is which vegetable?

    Okra, or ladies' fingers. The sliminess that puts people off is soluble fibre, and it disappears if you keep the pods dry and the pan hot.

  43. 43Karela is famously bitter. What is the usual way to tame it?

    Salt draws out the bitter juice; you wring it out and cook on. Plenty of cooks refuse to, on the grounds that the bitterness is the entire point.

  44. 44In 'chicken tikka', what does tikka mean?

    It just means bits or pieces. The cooking method is in the tandoor, not in the name.

  45. 45A thali is named after...

    The plate. A meal named after its crockery, which is a fair description of a format where the arrangement is as fixed as the food.

  46. 46Which country grows and eats the most pulses — dals, beans, chickpeas?

    India by a wide margin on both counts, and it still imports more, which is roughly how much dal gets eaten there.

  47. 47The green paste served with sushi outside Japan is usually...

    Real wasabi is difficult to grow and loses its punch within about fifteen minutes of grating, so most of the world has been eating dyed horseradish for decades.

  48. 48Which of these does not spoil, essentially ever?

    Low water, high acidity and a little hydrogen peroxide make honey hostile to microbes. Edible honey has been found in Egyptian tombs.

  49. 49Why does pineapple sting your tongue?

    Bromelain breaks down protein, and your tongue is protein. It is, briefly and very slightly, eating you back.

  50. 50Carrots were originally which colour?

    Purple and yellow, and India still grows red-black ones for kanji and gajar halwa. The orange carrot is a much later Dutch development.

  51. 51The cashew, so at home in Goan and Kerala cooking, came from...

    Portuguese traders brought it from Brazil in the 16th century. Feni, Goa's cashew spirit, is made from the fruit the nut hangs off, not the nut.

  52. 52Why do you never see cashews sold in the shell?

    The shell oil blisters skin, and shelling is skilled, hazardous work. That labour is a large part of what you are paying for.

  53. 53Kadhai paneer is named after...

    The deep, round-bottomed pan. Its shape concentrates the heat at the base, which is why the same dish in a flat frying pan never quite tastes right.

  54. 54Ghee keeps far longer than butter because...

    Water and milk protein are what spoil. Take both out and you have almost pure fat, which keeps for months without a fridge — the reason it exists.

  55. 55Indian tej patta and the European bay leaf are...

    Tej patta is cassia, closer to cinnamon. Swapping in a Mediterranean bay leaf gives you a completely different flavour and a mildly confused biryani.

  56. 56Chilling an onion before chopping helps because...

    Cutting releases enzymes that produce a volatile sulphur compound; cold slows the reaction and the gas rises less readily. A sharp knife helps too, by crushing fewer cells.

  57. 57India is the world's second largest producer of rice. Who is first?

    China grows more, but India exports far more, and has been the largest rice exporter for years.

  58. 58Why is naan hard to make properly at home?

    A tandoor runs around 400-480°C, roughly double a home oven. The blistering and the chew both come from cooking that fast. A very hot cast-iron pan is the usual compromise.

  59. 59What do sourdough bread and idli batter have in common?

    Same principle, different continents and grains. Both are also unreliable in a cold kitchen, and both are the reason cooks talk about their batter as though it has moods.

  60. 60Kala namak, black salt, smells strongly of what?

    Sulphur compounds give it a distinctly eggy note. It is also why it turns up in vegan cooking outside India, doing an impression of scrambled eggs.
