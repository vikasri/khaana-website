West India

Maharashtrian Cuisine

A coastal tradition and a plateau tradition, sharing one state, plus the street food that fed Mumbai's mill workers.

Konkan coast vs. Deccan plateau

Maharashtra's cuisine really splits into two distinct traditions defined by geography. Along the Konkan coast, the food looks a lot like its coastal neighbors Goa and Kerala: rice, coconut, and seafood, cooked with kokum (a tart dried fruit) for sourness. Inland, on the drier Deccan plateau, the cuisine shifts to millets (jowar and bajra) instead of rice, peanuts and sesame instead of coconut, and famously dry, intensely spiced curries. Kolhapuri cuisine, from the southern Deccan, is often cited as one of the spiciest regional styles in India.

The Maratha Empire and a warrior food culture

Maharashtra's political identity is inseparable from the Maratha Empire, built under Chhatrapati Shivaji in the 17th century and expanding into one of the dominant powers in India by the 18th. Practical, portable, high-energy foods associated with military campaigns, like dry chutneys, spice mixes (goda masala, kanda-lasun masala), and preserved pickles, became embedded in everyday cooking well beyond their original martial context.

Vada pav wasn't handed down through generations. It was invented on a specific street corner, for a specific economic reason, in 1966.

Mumbai and the invention of vada pav

The most-told food-history story from this region is a recent, well-documented one. Vada pav, a spiced potato fritter in a bread roll, is credited to a street vendor named Ashok Vaidya, who began selling it near Dadar railway station in 1966. It was explicitly designed as a cheap, fast, filling meal for Mumbai's textile mill workers, at a time when Western-style sandwiches were priced out of reach for that same crowd. It's a rare case in Indian food history where a genuinely "invented," rather than gradually evolved, dish can be traced to a specific person, place, and year, and it went on to become Mumbai's defining street food.

Quick facts

Region
Western India (Maharashtra): coastal Konkan & inland Deccan
Staple grain
Rice (coastal), jowar & bajra millets (inland)
Defining technique
Coconut-and-kokum coastal cooking; dry, intense Deccan spice blends
Key historical moment
Vada pav's invention near Dadar station, Mumbai, 1966
Signature dish
Vada pav

Signature dishes

  • Vada pav: a spiced potato fritter in a bread roll, invented in Mumbai in 1966.
  • Misal pav: a spicy sprouted-lentil curry topped with crunchy farsan, served with bread.
  • Puran poli: a sweet flatbread stuffed with a jaggery-lentil filling.
  • Modak: a steamed dumpling of rice flour, coconut, and jaggery, associated with the Ganesh Chaturthi festival.
  • Kolhapuri mutton: an intensely spiced, dry-roasted-masala curry from the southern Deccan.
