Andhra

Gongura Mamsam

mutton braised with sour sorrel leaves, the dish Andhra is judged by

dairy freeegg freegluten freenut free
Prep20 min
Cook70 min
Active90 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Allergens: none of the ten listed below

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Ingredients Method Nutrition

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 700g on the bone, 4cm pieces Muttonbone matters here, the gravy is thin without it
  • 300g leaves Amaranth Greensstanding in for gongura (sorrel); if you find gongura use it and cut the tamarind by half
  • 20g Tamarindonly to make up the sourness the substitute lacks
  • 3 medium, finely sliced Onion
  • 8 Dried Red ChilliGuntur if you can get them
  • 1 tbsp paste Ginger
  • 1 tbsp paste Garlic
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 2 tsp Coriander Powder
  • 5 tbsp Neutral Oil
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop, pressure cooker

Before you start

  • The greens go in twice. Half early, to melt into the gravy, half at the end so something still tastes green.
  • Gongura is aggressively sour. If you are using amaranth, the tamarind is doing the work the leaf would.

Method

  1. Wilt the greens in a dry pan until they collapse and give up their water, about 5 minutes. Set aside.No oil. You want them dry, not fried.
  2. Brown the onion in the oil until properly dark. Add the ginger and garlic pastes and cook out the raw smell.
  3. Add the mutton, turmeric, coriander powder and chillies. Fry on high until the meat is sealed and colouring.Do not add salt yet, it draws water and stews the meat.
  4. Add 250ml water and the tamarind, cover and cook 50 minutes until the mutton yields, or 5 whistles in a pressure cooker.
  5. Stir in half the wilted greens and simmer uncovered 10 minutes until the gravy tightens around the meat.
  6. Fold in the rest of the greens, salt to taste, and rest 10 minutes before serving with rice.

Safe temperatures: Whole cuts of mutton, beef and pork are done at 63°C / 145°F, rested 3 minutes. A long braise passes that well before the meat is tender. Reheat leftovers to 74°C / 165°F. FoodSafety.gov

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and improves overnight as the sourness settles into the meat. Reheat gently with a splash of water. Freezes well for a month.
