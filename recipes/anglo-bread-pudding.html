Anglo-Indian

Bread Pudding

stale bread, milk and dried fruit baked into the household dessert

no onion garlicnut free
Ingredients Method Nutrition
Prep20 min
Cook55 min
Active75 min
Plus20 min resting
Serves6
Difficultyeasy

Contains: milk, gluten, egg

Ingredients

Quantities for 6.

  • 300g stale, torn Breadstale is not a compromise, fresh bread turns to paste
  • 600ml Milk
  • 3 Eggs
  • 150g Sugar
  • 50g, melted Butter
  • 80g Raisins
  • 6 pods, seeds ground Cardamom
  • 1/4 tsp, grated Nutmeg
  • 60g, chopped Dried Apricotoptional

Cookware

oven

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Before you start

  • Every Anglo-Indian household made this and no two made it the same way. What is constant is stale bread, milk and whatever dried fruit was in the tin.
  • Soak the bread properly. Twenty minutes, not five.

Method

  1. Warm the milk with the sugar until it dissolves. Pour it over the torn bread and leave 20 minutes.
  2. Mash the soaked bread roughly with a fork. It should be lumpy, not smooth.
  3. Beat in the eggs, melted butter, cardamom and nutmeg, then fold in the raisins and apricot.
  4. Pour into a greased dish and bake at 180C for 50 minutes until set with a browned top and a slight wobble at the centre.A slight wobble. Baked until firm all through, it is dry.
  5. Cool 20 minutes before cutting. It firms as it settles.

Safe temperatures: Egg dishes are done at 71°C / 160°F; a whole egg when the yolk and white are both firm. Reheat leftovers to 74°C / 165°F. FoodSafety.gov

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated. Serve at room temperature or warmed. It contains egg and milk, so keep it cold rather than on the counter.
