Dak Bungalow Curry

the chicken curry cooked in colonial rest houses, thickened with a boiled egg and a potato

dairy freegluten freenut free
Ingredients Method Nutrition
Prep20 min
Cook50 min
Active70 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Contains: egg

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 800g on the bone Chicken
  • 3 medium, halved Potatofried before it goes in, which is what makes it a dak bungalow curry
  • 4, hard-boiled and peeled Eggs
  • 3, finely sliced Onion
  • 1 tbsp paste Ginger
  • 1 tbsp paste Garlic
  • 1.5 tsp, crushed Black Pepperpepper rather than chilli leads this curry
  • 1 tsp Garam Masala
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 2 Bay Leaf
  • 1 tbsp Vinegar
  • 5 tbsp Neutral Oil
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Before you start

  • Dak bungalows were the rest houses on the mail routes. The khansama cooked whatever was to hand, which is why the dish carries a potato and an egg.
  • Fry the potato and the boiled eggs before they go in. That browning is the difference between this and an ordinary curry.

Method

  1. Fry the halved potatoes in the oil until golden on all sides. Lift out. Fry the boiled eggs until blistered. Lift out.
  2. In the same oil, brown the onion deeply with the bay leaves. Add the ginger and garlic and cook out.
  3. Add the chicken, turmeric and half the pepper. Fry on high until sealed and coloured, 8 minutes.
  4. Add 400ml water, cover and simmer 25 minutes. Return the potatoes for the last 15.
  5. Add the eggs, the rest of the pepper, the garam masala and the vinegar. Simmer uncovered 5 minutes.The vinegar goes in at the end. Boiled long it just tastes sour.
  6. Rest 10 minutes before serving with bread or rice.

Safe temperatures: Chicken and duck are done at 74°C / 165°F, taken at the thickest part and clear of the bone. Egg dishes are done at 71°C / 160°F; a whole egg when the yolk and white are both firm. Reheat leftovers to 74°C / 165°F. FoodSafety.gov

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and is better on day two. Take the eggs out before freezing; boiled whites go rubbery.
