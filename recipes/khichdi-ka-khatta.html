Hyderabadi

Khichdi Ka Khatta

thin, sharp tamarind sauce poured over khichdi, sour first and sweet after

vegetarianvegangluten freedairy freenut free
Prep10 min
Cook20 min
Total30 min
Serves4
Difficultyeasy

Allergens: none of the common ones

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 60g block, soaked in 400ml hot water Tamarind
  • 8 cloves, lightly crushed Garlic
  • 4, slit Green Chilli
  • 2 sprigs Curry Leaves
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 1/2 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1/4 tsp Fenugreek Seedsa scant quarter; more turns it bitteroptional
  • 3, broken Dried Red Chilli
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep the dish gluten-freeoptional
  • 1/4 tsp Turmeric
  • 1 tsp Chilli Powderoptional
  • 2 tbsp, grated Jaggerybalances the sourness; the khatta should still lead with tamarind
  • 3 tbsp Neutral Oil
  • 2 tbsp, chopped Coriander Leavesoptional
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop

Before you start

  • Soak the tamarind in hot water for 20 minutes, then squeeze it out thoroughly with your fingers and strain. Do this before anything else.
  • Khatta is meant to be pourable, closer to rasam than to chutney. Have extra hot water ready in case it tightens too much.
  • Grate the jaggery before you start; a solid lump will not dissolve evenly in a thin sauce.

Method

  1. Squeeze the soaked tamarind hard in its water, strain through a sieve, and press the pulp to get everything out. Discard the fibres.
  2. Heat the oil in a pan and pop the mustard seeds, 20 seconds, until they stop jumping.
  3. Add the cumin, fenugreek seeds and dried red chillies and fry 15 seconds, no longer.Fenugreek burns in seconds and takes the whole pot bitter with it. Count it out.
  4. Add the asafoetida, curry leaves, crushed garlic and green chillies and fry 1 minute, until the garlic is pale gold and the leaves are crisp.
  5. Stir in the turmeric and chilli powder off the heat for 10 seconds so they bloom without scorching.
  6. Pour in the strained tamarind water and 200ml plain water, add the salt, and bring to a boil.
  7. Simmer uncovered 12 minutes, until the raw edge of the tamarind has gone and the sauce smells rounded rather than sharp.Undercooked tamarind tastes metallic. Twelve minutes is the minimum, and it is the difference between good khatta and puckering acid.
  8. Stir in the jaggery and simmer 3 minutes more, until dissolved. Taste: sour should lead, sweet should follow.
  9. Slacken with hot water if it has thickened past pouring consistency, scatter the coriander, and serve hot over khichdi.

Storage

Keeps 5 days refrigerated and the flavour improves after a day. Reheat on the stovetop, thinning with a little hot water as it will have thickened.
