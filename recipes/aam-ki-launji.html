Rajasthani

Aam ki Launji

raw mango cooked down with jaggery, fennel and nigella into a sweet-sour relish

vegetarianvegangluten freedairy freenut freeno onion garlic
Prep15 min
Cook25 min
Total40 min
Serves6
Difficultyeasy

Allergens: none of the common ones

Ingredients

Quantities for 6.

  • 500g, peeled and cut into 2cm chunks Raw Mangofirm and sour, not turning ripe
  • 180g, grated Jaggeryadjust to how sour the mangoes are
  • 2 tbsp Mustard Oil
  • 1.5 tsp Fennel Seeds
  • 1/2 tsp Nigella
  • 1/4 tsp Fenugreek Seedsjust a few seeds, for the bitter note
  • 1/2 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep this gluten-free
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 1 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 2, broken Dried Red Chillioptional
  • 3/4 tsp Salt
  • 200ml Water

Equipment

stovetop, kadhai

Before you start

  • Taste a sliver of the raw mango before you start. A very sour mango needs the full 180g of jaggery; a milder one needs closer to 120g.
  • Grate or chop the jaggery so it melts evenly — a solid lump sinks and scorches on the base of the pan.
  • Heat the mustard oil to smoking point and let it settle before adding the seeds, or the launji will taste raw and pungent.

Method

  1. Heat the mustard oil in a kadhai until it just smokes, then lower the heat and let it cool for 30 seconds.
  2. Add the fenugreek seeds first and let them go one shade darker, then the fennel, nigella and cumin. They should sizzle and smell toasty within 20 seconds.Fenugreek turns bitter fast — count to five and move on.
  3. Add the hing and broken red chillies, stir once, then tip in the mango chunks.
  4. Stir in the turmeric, chilli powder and salt and toss the mango in the oil for 3 minutes until the edges look glossy.
  5. Pour in 200ml water, cover, and cook on low heat 10 minutes until the mango is tender but the chunks still hold.Poke a piece with a knife tip. It should give without falling apart.
  6. Add the jaggery and stir until it melts completely. Uncover and simmer 8-10 minutes.
  7. Cook until the syrup coats the back of a spoon and a line drawn through it on the pan base closes slowly. It thickens further as it cools, so stop while it still looks slightly loose.Overcooked launji sets hard in the jar and cannot be rescued.
  8. Cool completely before jarring. Serve alongside dal baati, puri or paratha.

Storage

Cools into a thick relish that keeps 3 weeks refrigerated in a clean, dry jar, or 4 days at room temperature in high summer. Always use a dry spoon.
