Andhra

Natu Kodi Pulusu

country chicken in a thin tamarind gravy, cooked long enough to matter

dairy freeegg freegluten freenut free
Prep20 min
Cook60 min
Active80 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Allergens: none of the ten listed below

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Ingredients Method Nutrition

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 800g on the bone, large pieces Chickenfree-range or older bird if you can, it is the point of the dish
  • 40g, soaked in 300ml warm water Tamarind
  • 3 medium, sliced Onion
  • 2, chopped Tomato
  • 6 Dried Red Chilli
  • 2 tsp Coriander Powder
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 2 sprigs Curry Leaves
  • 5 tbsp Neutral Oil
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop, pressure cooker

Before you start

  • Pulusu means sour. The tamarind is not a background note, it is the dish.
  • Country chicken is tougher and better flavoured than broiler. If you use broiler, cut the cooking time by half.

Method

  1. Fry the onion in the oil until deep brown. Add the curry leaves and dried chillies.
  2. Add the chicken, turmeric and coriander powder and fry on high until the pieces colour, 8 minutes.
  3. Add the tomato and cook until it breaks down completely.
  4. Strain in the tamarind water, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer 40 minutes until the chicken is tender.It should stay thin. This is a gravy for pouring over rice, not for coating it.
  5. Season and rest 10 minutes. The sourness rounds out as it stands.

Safe temperatures: Chicken and duck are done at 74°C / 165°F, taken at the thickest part and clear of the bone. Reheat leftovers to 74°C / 165°F. FoodSafety.gov

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and tastes better on day two. The tamarind keeps it stable. Reheat to a full simmer.
