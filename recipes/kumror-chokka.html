Kumror Chokka

sweet pumpkin and chickpeas tempered with panch phoron, cooked until the edges collapse

dairy freeegg freegluten freehealthierno onion garlicnut freeveganvegetarian
Ingredients Method Nutrition
Prep20 min
Cook35 min
Active55 min
Plus8 hr soaking
Serves4
Difficultyeasy
Per serving353 kcal

Start the day before. The soaking alone is 8 hr, against 55 min of actual work.

Allergens in Kumror Chokka: mustard.

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 600g, peeled and cut into 3cm chunks Pumpkin
  • 100g dried, soaked overnight Chickpeasor 250g tinned, drained and rinsed
  • 2 medium, cut into 3cm chunks Potato
  • 1 tsp Panch Phoron
  • 2, broken in half Dried Red Chilli
  • 1 Bay Leaf
  • 1 tbsp, grated Ginger
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep the dish gluten-free
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 1/2 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 1 tsp Cumin Powder
  • 1 tsp Coriander Powder
  • 1 tsp Sugar
  • to taste Salt
  • 3 tbsp Mustard Oil
  • 3 tbsp, freshly grated Coconutfor the finishoptional
  • 1/2 tsp Garam Masalaoptional
  • 100ml, plus 2 tbsp for the masala Water

Cookware

stovetop, kadhai, pressure cooker

Ingredient names in English →

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Before you start

  • Soak the dried chickpeas overnight in plenty of water, then pressure-cook them with a little salt for 4 whistles. They should be tender but still hold their shape.
  • Choose a dense, sweet pumpkin. A watery one dissolves into soup; if yours is very ripe, cut the chunks a size larger.
  • Measure the panch phoron and chillies into a small bowl before you start. The tempering burns in seconds and there is no time to fetch things.

Method

  1. Heat the mustard oil in a kadhai to smoking point, then take it off the heat for half a minute to settle.
  2. Fry the potato chunks with a pinch of the turmeric over medium heat for 5 minutes, until the edges are golden, then lift them out.Frying the potato separately keeps it from breaking up later in the pumpkin.
  3. Into the same oil add the panch phoron, broken chillies and bay leaf and let them pop for about 20 seconds.
  4. Stir in the asafoetida and grated ginger and cook 30 seconds, until fragrant.
  5. Add the turmeric, chilli, cumin and coriander powders with 2 tbsp water and fry this paste for 2 minutes, until the oil beads at the edges.Spices added to a splash of water instead of dry oil will not scorch.
  6. Return the potato and add the pumpkin, cooked chickpeas, salt and sugar. Toss everything in the masala for 2 minutes.
  7. Pour in 100ml water, cover, and cook on low for 15 minutes until the pumpkin gives way under a spoon.
  8. Mash four or five pieces of pumpkin against the side of the pan to thicken the gravy, add the coconut and cook uncovered for 3 minutes.This is what makes it a chokka: half mash, half chunks.
  9. Sprinkle over the garam masala, cover and rest 5 minutes off the heat before serving with luchi or rice.

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated. Reheat gently in a pan with a splash of water; it is also good at room temperature the next morning.
