Sambharam

spiced buttermilk cooler with crushed ginger, chilli and curry leaf

egg freegluten freenut freevegetarian
Ingredients Method Nutrition
Prep10 min
Cook0 min
Active10 min
Serves4
Difficultyeasy

Contains: milk

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 250g, thick and fresh Yogurtsour yogurt makes the drink harsh
  • 750ml, chilled Water
  • 2 small, peeled ShallotKerala small onions, crushed not chopped
  • 2cm piece, peeled Ginger
  • 1, slit Green Chilliseeds removed if you want it mild
  • 10 Curry Leaves
  • 1 tbsp, chopped stems included Coriander Leavesoptional
  • 1 tsp juice Lemononly if the yogurt is very mildoptional
  • 1 small pinch Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep this gluten-freeoptional
  • 3/4 tsp, or to taste Salt

Cookware

blender

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Before you start

  • Chill the water and the yogurt for at least an hour. Sambharam is only good cold.
  • Use yogurt set that morning if you can; anything older turns the drink sharp.
  • Crush the aromatics in a stone mortar if you have one, not a blender, for a cleaner flavour.

Method

  1. Crush the shallot, ginger, green chilli and curry leaves together with the salt into a rough, wet paste.The salt gives the pestle something to grip, so the aromatics bruise instead of sliding around.
  2. Whisk the yogurt in a wide bowl for about 1 minute until it is completely smooth and pourable, with no lumps clinging to the whisk.Whisk the yogurt alone first. Adding water too early leaves permanent lumps.
  3. Pour in the chilled water in three additions, whisking between each, until you have a thin, milky liquid.
  4. Stir the crushed paste through the buttermilk along with the chopped coriander leaves.
  5. Add the pinch of asafoetida if using and stir once.
  6. Leave to stand 10 minutes at room temperature so the ginger and chilli bleed into the liquid.Any longer and the shallot starts to dominate. Strain it at the 10-minute mark if you are not serving right away.
  7. Taste and correct the salt, adding the lemon juice only if the drink tastes flat.
  8. Strain through a coarse sieve into a jug and serve over ice, or pour unstrained for a rougher, more rustic glass.

Storage

Best within 4 hours. Keeps 1 day covered in the fridge, but strain out the aromatics first or it turns bitter. Do not freeze.
