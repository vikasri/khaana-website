Bengali

Bengali Alur Dom

baby potatoes sealed in a tangy yogurt and ginger gravy, the standard partner to luchi

egg freegluten freehealthierno onion garlicnut freevegetarian
Prep15 min
Cook35 min
Active50 min
Plus20 min resting
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Contains: milk, mustard

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Ingredients Method Nutrition

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 600g baby potatoes, boiled and peeled Potato
  • 100g, whisked smooth Yogurt
  • 1 large, pureed Tomato
  • 1 tbsp, ground to a paste Ginger
  • 1.5 tsp Cumin Seeds1 tsp for the tempering, 1/2 tsp dry-roasted for the finish
  • 2 Dried Red Chillione for the tempering, one dry-roasted for the finish
  • 1 Bay Leaf
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep the dish gluten-free
  • 3/4 tsp Turmeric
  • 1.5 tsp Kashmiri Chilli
  • 1 tsp Cumin Powder
  • 1 tsp Coriander Powder
  • 1/2 tsp Garam Masala
  • 2, slit Green Chilli
  • 1 tsp Sugar
  • to taste Salt
  • 4 tbsp Mustard Oil
  • 1 tsp Gheeoptional
  • 200ml Water

Cookware

stovetop, kadhai

Before you start

  • Boil the potatoes only until a fork goes in with slight resistance, then peel and prick them all over with a fork so the gravy gets inside.
  • Dry-roast 1/2 tsp cumin seeds and one dried chilli in a pan until they smell toasty, then grind them to a coarse powder. This bhaja moshla goes in at the very end and is what makes it taste Bengali.
  • Whisk the yogurt smooth and leave it at room temperature for 20 minutes before you begin.

Method

  1. Heat the mustard oil to smoking point in a kadhai and let it settle off the heat for 30 seconds.
  2. Fry the boiled potatoes with 1/4 tsp turmeric and a pinch of salt over medium heat for 6 to 7 minutes, until the skins are blistered and gold all over, then lift them out.Dry the potatoes properly first or they will spit and refuse to colour.
  3. Add the bay leaf, 1 tsp cumin seeds and one dried chilli to the oil and let them crackle for 20 seconds, then stir in the asafoetida.
  4. Fry the ginger paste for 1 minute, until it loses its raw smell.
  5. Add the pureed tomato with the remaining turmeric, kashmiri chilli, cumin and coriander powders and cook 6 minutes, until the oil separates cleanly at the sides.
  6. Pull the pan off the heat, beat in the whisked yogurt, then set it back on the lowest flame and cook 3 minutes without boiling.If it looks grainy, take it off and whisk hard; it will come back together.
  7. Return the potatoes with the sugar, salt and slit chillies and turn them in the masala for 3 minutes so every one is coated.
  8. Add the water, bring to a simmer, then cover tightly and cook on the lowest heat for 12 minutes.A tight lid is the point of dum: the potatoes finish in trapped steam, not in liquid.
  9. Uncover, raise the heat and reduce for 3 minutes until the gravy clings to the potatoes rather than pooling.
  10. Stir through the garam masala, the ghee and the roasted bhaja moshla, cover and rest 5 minutes before serving.

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and tastes better on day two. Reheat on low with a splash of water and add the bhaja moshla fresh if you have it.
