Ambot Tik

a sharp coconut-free fish curry that is exactly what its name says, sour and hot

dairy freeegg freegluten freehealthiernut freepescatarian
Ingredients Method Nutrition
Prep20 min
Cook25 min
Active45 min
Plus15 min resting
Serves4
Difficultyeasy

Contains: fish

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 600g, firm, in 4cm chunks Fishtraditionally shark or catfish; kingfish or monkfish hold up well
  • 10 dried, stemmed Kashmiri Chilli
  • 4 hot ones Dried Red Chillithe tik half of the name
  • lime-sized ball, soaked in 200ml hot water Tamarind
  • 3 tbsp Vinegar
  • 5 petals Kokumthe second sour noteoptional
  • 10 cloves Garlic
  • 3cm piece Ginger
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1 tsp Black Pepper
  • 3/4 tsp Turmeric
  • 1 tbsp Coriander Powder
  • 2 medium, finely chopped Onion
  • 3 tbsp Neutral Oil
  • to taste Salt
  • 400ml Water

Cookware

stovetop, blender

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Before you start

  • Rub the fish with salt and 1/4 tsp turmeric and rest it 15 minutes. A firm fish takes salt slowly.
  • Soak the tamarind in hot water while you grind the masala, then squeeze and strain.
  • There is no coconut in this curry. Do not add any to soften it. The sharpness is the dish.

Method

  1. Grind the chillies, garlic, ginger, cumin, pepper, turmeric and coriander powder with the vinegar and 3 tbsp water to a fine paste.
  2. Heat the oil and fry the chopped onion over medium heat for 8 minutes, until soft and pale gold.
  3. Add the masala paste and fry 6-7 minutes until it darkens and the oil beads at the edge of the pan.Frying the vinegar out is what stops the curry tasting raw. Give it the full seven minutes.
  4. Pour in the tamarind water and the rest of the water, add the kokum and salt, and bring to a simmer.
  5. Simmer uncovered 10 minutes so the gravy reduces slightly and turns glossy.
  6. Taste and balance: it should be assertively sour and hot. Add a pinch of salt or a splash of vinegar rather than sugar.Balance it before the fish goes in. You cannot stir it properly afterwards.
  7. Slide in the fish, shake the pan to settle the pieces, and simmer 7-8 minutes until opaque through.
  8. Rest 10 minutes off the heat before serving with plain rice.

Safe temperatures: Fish is done at 63°C / 145°F, or when it turns opaque and flakes easily. Reheat leftovers to 74°C / 165°F. FoodSafety.gov

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and the sourness mellows usefully by day two. Reheat gently to a bare simmer; never boil hard or the fish shreds.
