Ragi Sangati

finger millet and rice pounded into a smooth ball, the working staple of Rayalaseema

dairy freeegg freegluten freenut freeveganvegetarian
Prep10 min
Cook30 min
Active40 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Allergens: none of the ten listed below

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Ingredients Method Nutrition

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 200g Ragi Flour
  • 100g, washed Ricecooked soft with the millet, not separately
  • 1.2 litres Water
  • 1/2 tsp Salt

Cookware

stovetop

Before you start

  • Sangati is eaten with a hot, thin curry, usually natu kodi pulusu or a chilli and onion chutney. On its own it is deliberately plain.
  • The pounding is the technique. A whisk gives lumps; a sturdy wooden spoon and some force gives the smooth, heavy texture the dish is named for.

Method

  1. Boil the rice in the water until completely soft and collapsing, about 20 minutes. Do not drain it.
  2. Lower the heat. Rain the ragi flour in with one hand while stirring hard with the other.All at once and you get a lump you will never break up.
  3. Cook 8 minutes, stirring constantly, until it pulls away from the sides of the pan.
  4. Take it off the heat and pound it against the side of the pan with a wooden spoon until glossy and smooth, about 3 minutes.
  5. Wet your hands and shape into balls. Serve at once with a hot curry.It sets firm as it cools, so shape while hot.

Storage

Best eaten straight away. It keeps a day refrigerated but sets hard; steam it briefly to soften rather than microwaving, which dries the surface.
