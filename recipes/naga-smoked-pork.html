Naga Smoked Pork with Bamboo Shoot

Smoked pork simmered with fermented bamboo shoot and raja mircha

dairy freeegg freegluten freehealthiernut free
Ingredients Method Nutrition
Prep15 min
Cook75 min
Active90 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Allergens: none of the ten listed below

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 700g smoked belly, in cubes Pork
  • 1 cup fermented, rinsed Bamboo Shoot
  • 4 Green Chilliraja mircha traditionally, use far less
  • 2 tbsp Ginger
  • 8 cloves Garlic
  • 1, sliced Onionoptional
  • 1 Tomatooptional
  • to finish Coriander Leavesoptional
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Before you start

  • Naga cooking uses very little oil and few spices; the flavour is carried by smoke, fermentation and heat. Adding garam masala here would be a mistake.
  • Fermented bamboo shoot is pungent. Rinse it, and add it in stages, tasting as you go.

Method

  1. Rinse the fermented bamboo shoot in two changes of water and drain.
  2. Put the smoked pork in a pot with the ginger, garlic, chillies and just enough water to cover. Bring to a boil.No oil. The pork renders plenty of its own.
  3. Simmer covered 45-60 minutes until the pork is tender and the water has reduced by half.
  4. Add the bamboo shoot and the onion and tomato if using, and cook a further 15 minutes.Taste before adding all the bamboo. Its sourness varies enormously between batches.
  5. Season with salt only at the end; smoked pork is already salty.
  6. Serve with plain rice and a raw chilli on the side.

Safe temperatures: Whole cuts of mutton, beef and pork are done at 63°C / 145°F, rested 3 minutes. A long braise passes that well before the meat is tender. Reheat leftovers to 74°C / 165°F. FoodSafety.gov

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and improves. Freezes 2 months.
