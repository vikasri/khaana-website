Northeast Indian

Sel Roti

crisp-edged ring of fried fermented rice batter, lightly sweet and cardamom-scented

vegetariangluten freenut free
Prep25 min
Cook30 min
Total55 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Contains: dairy

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 500g raw non-basmati rice Rice
  • 120g Sugar
  • 3 tbsp, melted and cooled Ghee
  • 6 pods, seeds ground fine Cardamom
  • 60ml Milksome households use water; milk gives a softer crumb inside the crisp shell
  • about 150ml, for adjusting the batter Water
  • 1/2 tsp Salt
  • 1 litre, for deep frying Neutral Oil
  • 2, ground Clovesoptional

Equipment

stovetop, kadhai, blender

Before you start

  • Soak the rice in cold water for at least 8 hours, or overnight. It has to be fully softened or the batter will be gritty.
  • The batter then rests 3-4 hours in a warm spot to ferment. Plan for most of a day, or soak overnight and fry in the afternoon.
  • Have a wide, deep pan and a long skewer or chopstick ready before you start frying — the rings are turned with the skewer, not tongs.

Method

  1. Drain the soaked rice thoroughly and spread it on a cloth for 15 minutes until the surface looks dry.Wet rice means a slack batter, and slack batter will not hold a ring in hot oil.
  2. Grind the rice in batches with the milk and only as much of the water as it takes to move the blades, to a thick, completely smooth batter.Grind longer rather than adding water. The batter should fall from a spoon in a heavy ribbon.
  3. Beat in the sugar, melted ghee, ground cardamom, cloves and salt, then beat by hand for a good 5 minutes.That beating aerates the batter, and aeration is what gives sel roti its light inside.
  4. Cover and leave in a warm place for 3-4 hours, until the batter has risen slightly and smells faintly sweet and sour.
  5. Heat the oil in a kadhai to about 170C — a drop of batter should sink briefly, then rise within 3 seconds.Too hot and the ring browns before the middle sets; too cool and it drinks oil and goes heavy.
  6. Pour the batter into a squeeze bottle or a piping bag. Working quickly, pipe a 12cm ring onto the surface of the oil, joining the ends.
  7. Let it fry undisturbed 1 minute, until it floats free and the top loses its shine.
  8. Turn it with a skewer through the hole and fry another 2 minutes until both sides are a deep reddish gold.
  9. Drain on a wire rack, not paper, and fry the rest one or two at a time.
  10. Cool at least 10 minutes before eating — the crispness only arrives as they cool.

Storage

Keeps 3 days at room temperature in a tin, and stays edible for a week. Refresh in a dry pan over low heat for a minute a side; do not refrigerate, which makes them leathery.
