Karnataka

Baimbale Curry

tender monsoon bamboo shoot in coconut gravy, sour and faintly bitter

vegetarianvegangluten freedairy freenut freehealthier
Prep25 min
Cook40 min
Total65 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Allergens: none of the common ones

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 400g tender shoots, cleaned and finely chopped Bamboo Shootfresh monsoon shoots are traditional; brined shoots work if rinsed well
  • 1 cup fresh grated Coconut
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 3 Green Chilli
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 1 large, finely chopped Onion
  • 8 cloves, crushed Garlic
  • 2 sprigs Curry Leaves
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 2, broken Dried Red Chilli
  • 2 tsp Coriander Powder
  • 1 tsp, crushed Black Pepper
  • 3 tbsp Coconut Oil
  • 1 tbsp Vinegarstands in for kachampuli, the dark Coorg sour vinegar; a small amount goes in at the end and is what makes this curry taste Kodava
  • to taste Salt
  • 500 ml Water

Equipment

stovetop, kadhai, blender

Before you start

  • Fresh bamboo shoot must be boiled before it is fit to eat. Chop it, cover with water, boil 10 minutes, drain, and repeat twice more with fresh water — this removes the bitterness and the natural cyanogens.
  • If you are using brined or canned shoots, rinse them under running water for a minute and boil once for 5 minutes.
  • Grate the coconut while the shoots boil.

Method

  1. Boil the chopped bamboo shoot in plenty of water for 10 minutes, drain, and repeat twice with fresh water each time.Do not skip or shorten this. Under-boiled fresh bamboo is harshly bitter and genuinely not safe to eat.
  2. Grind the coconut, cumin and green chilli with 150 ml water to a smooth paste and set aside.
  3. Heat the coconut oil in a heavy pan, add the mustard seeds and let them pop, about 30 seconds.
  4. Add the dried red chilli and curry leaves and crackle for 10 seconds.
  5. Add the onion and garlic and fry until the onion is soft and gold at the edges, about 8 minutes.
  6. Stir in the turmeric, coriander powder and crushed pepper and fry 1 minute.
  7. Add the drained bamboo shoot and fry 5 minutes, stirring, until it smells sweet rather than sharp.
  8. Pour in 200 ml water and 1 tsp salt, cover and simmer 12 minutes until the shoot is tender to the bite.
  9. Stir in the ground coconut paste and simmer very gently 8 minutes; the gravy should thicken and turn glossy.Keep it below a rolling boil or the coconut will split.
  10. Take the pan off the heat, stir in the vinegar, cover and rest 5 minutes. Serve with hot rice.Add half the vinegar first and taste — the sourness should sit just behind the bamboo, not in front of it.

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and tastes better on day two. Reheat over low heat with a splash of water.
