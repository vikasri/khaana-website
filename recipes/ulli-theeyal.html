Ulli Theeyal

dark roasted-coconut and shallot curry, sour with tamarind

dairy freeegg freegluten freenut freeveganvegetarian
Ingredients Method Nutrition
Prep20 min
Cook40 min
Active60 min
Plus20 min soaking
Serves4
Difficultymoderate
Per serving237 kcal

Allergens in Ulli Theeyal: mustard.

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 300g, peeled and left whole Shallotsmall ones; halve any larger than a walnut
  • 1 cup grated, fresh or thawed frozen Coconutthe whole dish depends on roasting this properly
  • 2 tbsp Coriander Powder
  • 1.5 tsp Kashmiri Chillifor colour without heat
  • 1/2 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 1/4 tsp Fenugreek Seedsmore than this turns the gravy bitter
  • 1/2 tsp, coarsely cracked Black Pepperoptional
  • lime-sized ball, soaked in 200ml warm water Tamarind
  • 2 sprigs Curry Leaves
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 2, broken Dried Red Chilli
  • 1 tsp, grated Jaggeryrounds off the tamarindoptional
  • 3 tbsp Coconut Oil
  • 500ml Water
  • 1 tsp, or to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop, kadhai, blender

Ingredient names in English →

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Before you start

  • Soak the tamarind in 200ml warm water for 20 minutes, then squeeze and strain; discard the fibres.
  • Peel the shallots ahead, 300g takes a good 15 minutes and this is the slow part of the recipe.
  • Have the ground spices measured into one bowl before you start roasting the coconut; it goes from brown to burnt in seconds.

Method

  1. Heat 1 tbsp of the coconut oil in a heavy pan and roast the grated coconut over medium-low heat, stirring almost constantly, for 12-15 minutes until it is a deep even brown and smells nutty.Do not walk away. The coconut sits pale for eight minutes and then colours all at once.
  2. Add the fenugreek seeds and roast 30 seconds more, then take the pan off the heat.
  3. Stir the coriander powder, kashmiri chilli, chilli powder and turmeric into the hot coconut off the heat and let the residual warmth cook them for 1 minute.
  4. Cool completely, then grind with 150ml water to a very smooth, dark paste. A full 3-4 minutes in the blender.Any grit left in the paste stays grit in the finished curry. Grind longer than you think you need to.
  5. Heat the remaining 2 tbsp coconut oil in the same pan and fry the whole shallots over medium heat for 8 minutes until browned at the edges and softened.
  6. Pour in the strained tamarind water and 350ml water, add the salt, and simmer uncovered 10 minutes until the shallots are tender to a knife tip.
  7. Stir in the ground coconut paste, rinse the blender with a splash of water and add that too.
  8. Simmer gently 12-15 minutes, uncovered, until the gravy darkens and thickens enough to coat a spoon and oil beads at the surface.The oil separating at the edges is the sign it is done. Rushing this leaves a raw, chalky taste.
  9. Stir in the jaggery and cracked pepper, taste, and correct salt and sourness.
  10. In a small pan heat a teaspoon of coconut oil, crackle the mustard seeds, add the dried chillies and curry leaves, and pour the tempering over the curry.Cover the pan for a second as the curry leaves go in, they spit hard.
  11. Rest 10 minutes off the heat before serving with rice.

Storage

Keeps 4 days refrigerated and genuinely improves on the second day. Reheat gently on the stovetop with a splash of water; do not boil hard or the coconut paste splits.
