Dali Thoy

the everyday konkani toor dal, thin and clean, finished with hing and green chilli

dairy freeegg freegluten freehealthierno onion garlicnut freeveganvegetarian
Ingredients Method Nutrition
Prep10 min
Cook30 min
Active40 min
Plus20 min soaking
Serves4
Difficultyeasy

Contains: mustard

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 3/4 cup Toor Dal
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 3, slit lengthways Green Chilli
  • 1/2 inch piece, crushed Gingernot strictly traditional but common in Mangalore homesoptional
  • 1 1/2 tbsp Coconut Oilthe flavour of this dal is really coconut oil and hing, so use both properly
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 1/2 tsp Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep this gluten-free
  • 1 sprig Curry Leaves
  • 1, broken Dried Red Chillioptional
  • 2 tbsp, chopped Coriander Leavesoptional
  • 3 cups Water
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop, pressure cooker

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Before you start

  • Rinse the toor dal until the water runs clear. Cloudy dal makes a muddy thoy.
  • No soaking needed, but 20 minutes in warm water shaves cooking time if you are in a hurry.
  • Have the tempering ingredients within reach of the stove; the whole tadka takes under a minute.

Method

  1. Pressure cook the dal with 2 1/2 cups water and the turmeric for 4 whistles, then let the pressure fall on its own.
  2. Open the cooker and whisk the dal smooth, adding up to 1/2 cup hot water. Thoy should pour like thin cream, not sit in a mound.This is the single thing people get wrong. Thoy is meant to be thin enough to drink from the bowl.
  3. Add the green chillies, crushed ginger and salt and simmer 5 minutes on low.
  4. Taste and check the salt now, while there is still time for it to dissolve evenly.
  5. Heat the coconut oil in a small pan until it shimmers, then add the mustard seeds and let them pop fully, about 20 seconds.Wait for the popping to actually stop. Half-popped mustard tastes raw and slightly bitter.
  6. Take the pan off the flame, add the broken red chilli, curry leaves and hing, and let them foam in the residual heat for 5 seconds.Hing burns almost instantly on a live flame. Off the heat is the only safe way.
  7. Pour the tempering into the dal, cover immediately and leave it 2 minutes to trap the aroma.
  8. Stir through the coriander leaves and serve hot over rice with a spoon of coconut oil and a piece of pickle.

Storage

Keeps 2 days refrigerated. It thickens in the fridge, so whisk in hot water and re-warm gently; the tempering flattens after a day, so a fresh 20-second tadka on top is worth it.
