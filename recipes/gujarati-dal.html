Gujarati

Gujarati Dal

thin toor dal balanced sweet with jaggery and sharp with tamarind

vegetariangluten freeno onion garlic
Prep10 min
Cook30 min
Total40 min
Serves4
Difficultyeasy

Contains: dairy, nuts

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 150g Toor Dal
  • 900ml, plus more to thin Water
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 2.5 tbsp, grated Jaggery
  • 2 tbsp thick pulp Tamarind
  • 3 tbsp, raw Peanutcooked in with the dal until soft
  • 1 medium, chopped Tomatooptional
  • 1 tsp, crushed Ginger
  • 2, slit Green Chilli
  • 12 Curry Leaves
  • 2 tbsp Ghee
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1/4 tsp Fenugreek Seeds
  • 2cm piece Cinnamonoptional
  • 3 Clovesoptional
  • 2, broken Dried Red Chilli
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep this gluten-free
  • 1/2 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 3 tbsp, chopped Coriander Leavesoptional
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop, pressure cooker

Before you start

  • Soak the toor dal 30 minutes; it cooks more evenly and the peanuts soften alongside it.
  • Soak the tamarind in 4 tbsp hot water for 15 minutes and squeeze out the pulp, or use ready-made concentrate at half the quantity.
  • This dal is meant to be drinkably thin. Thinner than any north Indian dal. Keep hot water at hand to loosen it.

Method

  1. Pressure cook the drained dal with the peanuts, turmeric and 700ml water for 4 whistles, then let the pressure drop naturally.The peanuts must go in from the start; added later they stay hard.
  2. Whisk the cooked dal smooth and thin it with the remaining water until it pours easily from a ladle.
  3. Add the tamarind pulp, jaggery, tomato, ginger, green chilli and salt and simmer 12 minutes.
  4. Taste and adjust. It should read sweet, then sour, then salty, with heat arriving last.Balance it while it is thin; a thick dal tastes sweeter than it is and misleads you.
  5. Heat the ghee in a small pan and pop the mustard seeds.
  6. Add the cumin, fenugreek, cinnamon, cloves and dried red chilli and let them darken 15 seconds.
  7. Add the curry leaves, chilli powder and asafoetida off the heat, then pour the whole tempering into the dal.Off the heat for the chilli powder, or it turns black and bitter.
  8. Simmer 3 minutes more, scatter the coriander over and serve with rice and a spoon of ghee.

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and tastes better on day two. Reheat with plenty of extra water. It thickens considerably in the fridge.
