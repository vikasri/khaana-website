Chepala Pulusu

fish simmered in a dark tamarind gravy sharpened with fenugreek

pescatariangluten freedairy freenut freehealthier
Prep20 min
Cook30 min
Total50 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Contains: fish

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 600g firm steaks such as seer or kingfish, cut 2cm thick Fisha firm fish that will not fall apart; soft fillets disintegrate in the gravy
  • 60g, seedless Tamarind
  • 2 medium, finely chopped Onion
  • 1 large, chopped Tomato
  • 10 cloves, crushed Garlic
  • 2cm piece, grated Ginger
  • 3, slit Green Chilli
  • 2.5 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 1 tbsp Coriander Powder
  • 3/4 tsp Turmeric
  • 1/2 tsp Fenugreek Seedsdry-roasted and powdered. The signature note of a pulusu
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 3, broken Dried Red Chilli
  • 2 sprigs Curry Leaves
  • 1 tbsp Rice Flourslaked in 3 tbsp cold water to thicken the gravyoptional
  • 2 tsp, grated Jaggeryoptional
  • 4 tbsp Sesame Oil
  • a handful, chopped Coriander Leavesoptional
  • 1.5 tsp, or to taste Salt
  • 500ml Water

Cookware

stovetop, kadhai

Before you start

  • Rub the fish with 1/4 tsp turmeric and 1/2 tsp salt and refrigerate 20 minutes, then bring it back to room temperature before it goes into the pan.
  • Soak the tamarind in 300ml hot water for 25 minutes and extract a thick, dark pulp.
  • Dry-roast the fenugreek seeds until deep brown and powder them. Do this first, while the pan is still cool enough to control.

Method

  1. Heat the sesame oil in a wide kadhai and crackle the mustard seeds, then add the cumin, dried red chillies and curry leaves.
  2. Add the onion and cook 10 minutes over medium heat until soft and golden brown.
  3. Stir in the garlic, ginger and green chillies and fry 2 minutes until fragrant.
  4. Add the tomato and cook 6 minutes until it collapses completely.
  5. Add the chilli powder, coriander powder and remaining turmeric with 2 tbsp water and fry 2 minutes until the paste darkens to brick red.Keep the heat low here. Chilli powder scorches fast and a burnt pulusu cannot be rescued.
  6. Pour in the tamarind pulp and 500ml water, add the salt and jaggery, and boil hard for 12 minutes to cook off the raw tamarind edge.Twelve minutes of real boiling, not a lazy simmer. This is what turns sharp tamarind into a rounded gravy.
  7. Stir in the rice flour slurry and cook 2 minutes until the gravy thickens enough to coat a spoon.
  8. Slide the fish steaks in in a single layer, spoon gravy over them, and simmer uncovered on low heat for 8 to 10 minutes.Never stir once the fish is in. Tilt and swirl the pan instead, or you will end up with fish flakes in soup.
  9. Sprinkle over the fenugreek powder, shake the pan once, and take it off the heat.
  10. Scatter the coriander leaves and leave it to stand at least 2 hours before serving.A pulusu is genuinely better hours later, once the fish has drunk the gravy.

Storage

Keeps 2 days refrigerated and tastes best the next day. Reheat gently over low heat without stirring. Do not freeze. The fish turns woolly.
