Chettinad Kara Kuzhambu

fiery tamarind gravy with drumstick, brinjal and whole shallots

dairy freeegg freegluten freehealthiernut freeveganvegetarian
Prep20 min
Cook35 min
Total55 min
Serves4
Difficultyeasy

Contains: sesame, mustard

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 2, cut into 7cm lengths Drumstick
  • 4 small purple brinjals, quartered lengthways but left joined at the stem Eggplant
  • 15 small, peeled and left whole Shallot
  • 10 cloves, peeled and left whole Garlic
  • 1 medium, chopped Tomato
  • lime-sized ball, soaked in 500ml warm water Tamarind
  • 2 tbsp Sambar Powderor 1 tbsp chilli powder plus 1 tbsp coriander powder
  • 1 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 1 tsp, coarsely cracked Black Pepper
  • 1/2 tsp Fenugreek Seeds
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 1/2 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1/2 tsp Fennel Seeds
  • 1 tsp Toor Dalfried in the tempering for nuttinessoptional
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep this gluten-free
  • 2 sprigs Curry Leaves
  • 3, broken Dried Red Chilli
  • 5 tbsp Sesame Oilgingelly oil, and be generous. It is the body of the dish
  • 1 tsp, grated Jaggeryto round the sournessoptional
  • to taste Salt
  • 150ml Water

Cookware

stovetop, kadhai

Before you start

  • Soak the tamarind in 500ml warm water for 20 minutes, then extract and strain it.
  • Keep the quartered brinjals in water until the moment they go in, or they will darken.
  • Peel the shallots and garlic whole. This kuzhambu is about biting into soft whole aromatics, not a smooth gravy.

Method

  1. Heat the gingelly oil in a kadhai and add the mustard seeds; when they pop, add the toor dal, fenugreek, cumin and fennel.
  2. Fry 30 seconds until the dal is golden, then add the asafoetida, red chillies and curry leaves.The fenugreek should stay pale gold. Browned fenugreek makes the whole pot bitter.
  3. Add the whole shallots and garlic and fry 8 minutes over medium heat until browned and softening.Take the full eight minutes. Half-cooked shallots stay squeaky in the finished gravy.
  4. Add the tomato and cook 4 minutes until it breaks down into the oil.
  5. Lower the heat, add the sambar powder, chilli powder, turmeric and cracked pepper and fry 1 minute.
  6. Add the drumstick pieces and turn them in the masala for 2 minutes.
  7. Pour in the tamarind extract and 150ml water, season with salt and bring to a rolling boil.
  8. Simmer uncovered 12 minutes until the raw tamarind smell is gone and the gravy has darkened.
  9. Add the brinjals and simmer a further 8 minutes until they are tender but still holding their shape.
  10. Stir in the jaggery and simmer 2 minutes more, until the oil floats clear on top.That ring of oil on the surface is the sign it is done. Stop before it and the kuzhambu tastes thin.
  11. Rest 15 minutes off the heat and serve with rice and a spoonful of the oil from the top.

Storage

Keeps 4 days refrigerated. The sesame oil and tamarind preserve it well. Reheat on the stovetop; it thickens as it sits, so loosen with a little hot water.
