Gongura Pachadi

fierce sour-leaf chutney, eaten by the spoonful with hot rice

dairy freeegg freegluten freenut freeveganvegetarian
Prep15 min
Cook20 min
Active35 min
Plus20 min soaking
Serves6
Difficultyeasy

Contains: sesame, mustard

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Ingredients Method Nutrition

Ingredients

Quantities for 6.

  • 400g leaves, thick stems pulled off Amaranth Greensstanding in for gongura (sorrel), which is not in the catalogue; amaranth has the same soft body but none of the sourness, so the tamarind below is doing that job
  • 50g, seedless Tamarindnot optional here. It replaces the acidity real gongura would bring
  • 8 whole, stems snapped off Dried Red ChilliGuntur chillies if you have them
  • 4, roughly broken Green Chilli
  • 10 cloves, peeled Garlic
  • 5 tbsp Sesame OilIndian toasted gingelly oil, not the East Asian kind
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1/4 tsp Fenugreek Seedsa quarter teaspoon and no more
  • 1/4 tsp Turmeric
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep this gluten-freeoptional
  • 2 sprigs Curry Leavesoptional
  • 2 tsp, or to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop, kadhai, blender

Before you start

  • Soak the tamarind in 100ml of hot water for 20 minutes, then squeeze it to a thick pulp and discard the fibres.
  • Wash the greens and spread them on a cloth until bone dry. Wet leaves steam instead of frying and the chutney turns watery.
  • Have a clean, completely dry glass jar ready; any moisture and this will not keep.

Method

  1. Heat 2 tbsp of the sesame oil in a kadhai and fry the dried red chillies, fenugreek seeds and half the cumin over low heat for 2 minutes, until the chillies darken by a shade and smell toasty.Fenugreek burns from tan to black in about ten seconds. Pull the pan off the moment it colours.
  2. Lift the spices out onto a plate and set them aside to cool completely.
  3. In the same pan add another 2 tbsp oil and the garlic, and fry 2 minutes until the cloves are pale gold at the edges.
  4. Add the greens a large handful at a time with the green chillies and turmeric, and cook over medium-high heat for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring, until the leaves collapse and the pan goes almost dry.Keep cooking past the point where they look done. Any water left behind will spoil the chutney within days.
  5. Stir in the tamarind pulp and salt and cook another 4 minutes, until the mixture pulls away from the sides of the pan in a single mass.
  6. Cool the greens fully, then grind them with the toasted spices to a coarse paste. No water at all, and it should still have some texture.Grind in short pulses. A smooth purée loses the rustic bite this is meant to have.
  7. Heat the last tbsp of oil, crackle the mustard seeds and remaining cumin, add the curry leaves and asafoetida, and pour the whole tempering over the chutney.Wait for the mustard to actually pop before the curry leaves go in, or the tadka tastes raw.
  8. Fold the tempering through and taste for salt and sourness. It should hit sour first, then heat.

Storage

Packed into a dry jar with a thin film of oil on top, it keeps 3 weeks refrigerated. Always use a dry spoon. It is traditionally left out for several days and the sourness of the gongura does a lot of the work, but that is custom rather than a tested figure, so keep it in the fridge and take out what you need. Serve at room temperature.
