Bharli Vangi

baby aubergines stuffed with roasted peanut, coconut and goda masala, simmered in their own gravy

dairy freeegg freegluten freehealthierveganvegetarian
Prep25 min
Cook30 min
Active55 min
Plus15 min soaking
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Contains: peanuts, sesame

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Ingredients Method Nutrition

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 500g small round aubergines (about 8), stems left on Eggplant
  • 75g, roasted and ground Peanut
  • 40g, grated and dry-roasted Dried Coconutkhobra; the roasting is what makes the masala dark
  • 2 tbsp, dry-roasted Sesame
  • 2 medium, 1 chopped for the masala and 1 for the gravy Onion
  • 8 cloves Garlic
  • 3cm piece Ginger
  • 2 tsp Garam Masalastand-in for goda masala; use goda masala if you can get it
  • 2 tsp Coriander Powder
  • 2 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 1 tbsp thick pulp Tamarind
  • 1 tbsp, grated Jaggery
  • 5 tbsp Groundnut Oil
  • 300ml Water
  • 4 tbsp, chopped Coriander Leavesoptional
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop, kadhai

Before you start

  • Slit the aubergines into a cross from the base, stopping 1cm short of the stem, and drop them into salted water while you make the masala.
  • Roast the peanut, dried coconut and sesame separately. They brown at completely different speeds.
  • Soak the tamarind in 3 tbsp hot water for 15 minutes and squeeze out the pulp.

Method

  1. Dry-roast the grated dried coconut over low heat for 4 minutes until it is a deep golden brown, then tip it straight out of the pan.It goes from golden to burnt in about fifteen seconds. Do not walk away.
  2. Grind the roasted coconut, peanut and sesame with one chopped onion, the garlic and the ginger to a coarse, thick masala using 3 tbsp water.
  3. Mix the masala with the goda masala, coriander powder, chilli powder, turmeric, half the tamarind pulp, the jaggery and salt.
  4. Drain the aubergines, pat dry and pack about a tablespoon of masala into each slit, keeping the fruit whole. Reserve the leftover masala.Stuff them full but do not force the slits open. A split aubergine falls apart in the pan.
  5. Heat the oil in a wide pan and lay the stuffed aubergines in a single layer. Fry 6 minutes, turning them by shaking the pan, until the skins blister and dull.
  6. Lift the aubergines out, add the second onion, sliced, and cook 5 minutes until soft.
  7. Stir in the reserved masala and fry 4 minutes until it darkens and oil separates at the edges.
  8. Add the water and the remaining tamarind, return the aubergines to the pan, cover and simmer 20 minutes over low heat.Turn them by shaking the pan, never with a spoon. They are soft and will collapse.
  9. Check that a knife slides into an aubergine without resistance, and that the gravy is thick and rust-coloured with oil floating on top.
  10. Scatter with coriander and rest 10 minutes before serving with bhakri or rice.

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and improves overnight. Reheat covered over low heat with 3 tbsp water, shaking rather than stirring.
