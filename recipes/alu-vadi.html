Alu Vadi

colocasia leaves rolled with sweet-sour gram flour paste, steamed and sliced

dairy freeegg freegluten freeno onion garlicveganvegetarian
Ingredients Method Nutrition
Prep40 min
Cook30 min
Active70 min
Plus30 min cooling
Serves4
Difficultyadvanced

Contains: peanuts, sesame, mustard

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 12 large leaves (alu paan), the leaves not the corm, stems and thick veins shaved flat Colocasia
  • 1.5 cups (150g), sieved Besan
  • 2 tbsp Rice Flourhelps the rolls hold when slicedoptional
  • 3 tbsp thick pulp Tamarindnot optional. The acid is what stops the leaves itching
  • 3 tbsp, grated Jaggery
  • 1.5 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 1 tbsp Coriander Powder
  • 1 tsp Cumin Powder
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep this gluten-free
  • 2 tbsp in total Sesame
  • 3-4 tbsp, only if needed Wateroptional
  • 6 tbsp in total Groundnut Oil
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 3 tbsp, fresh grated Coconutto finishoptional
  • 3 tbsp, chopped Coriander Leavesto finishoptional
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop, steamer, kadhai

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Before you start

  • Soak a lime-sized ball of tamarind in 6 tbsp warm water for 20 minutes and squeeze out a thick pulp. Thin, watery tamarind will not do. It is both the flavour and the thing that neutralises the calcium oxalate that makes colocasia itch.
  • Wash and dry the leaves completely, then shave the raised veins flat with a knife held almost parallel to the leaf so the rolls fold without cracking.
  • The steamed rolls must cool fully, about 30 minutes, before slicing, or they will fall apart.

Method

  1. Mix the besan, rice flour, tamarind pulp, jaggery, chilli powder, coriander powder, cumin powder, turmeric, asafoetida, 1 tbsp sesame and salt into a thick, spreadable paste, adding water a teaspoon at a time only if it is too stiff.It should be the consistency of soft butter. Spreadable but not runny, or it will slide out of the rolls while steaming.
  2. Lay a leaf shiny side down, tip pointing away from you, and spread a thin, even layer of paste over the whole surface.
  3. Lay a second leaf on top facing the opposite way, spread again, and repeat until you have a stack of 3 leaves.Alternating the direction of the leaves gives you the spiral pattern when you slice.
  4. Fold in both long sides by 3cm, spread paste on the folded edges, then roll from the wide end into a tight cylinder. Seal the end with paste. Make 4 rolls.
  5. Steam the rolls over rolling water 25-30 minutes, until a knife goes through cleanly and the outside is a dull olive green.
  6. Cool completely, 30 minutes, then cut into 1.5cm slices with a sharp thin knife.
  7. Heat 4 tbsp oil in a kadhai and shallow-fry the slices in batches, 2 minutes a side, until the edges crisp and brown.
  8. Heat the remaining oil, pop the mustard seeds with the remaining sesame, and pour over the fried slices.
  9. Finish with fresh coconut and coriander and eat warm.If your mouth tingles at all, the tamarind was too weak. A squeeze of lemon over the slices fixes it.

Storage

Steamed rolls keep 4 days refrigerated and freeze for a month whole; slice and fry from chilled. Fried slices are best eaten within a few hours.
