Pork Vindaloo

Vinegar-and-garlic pork, descended from Portuguese carne de vinha d'alhos

gluten freedairy freenut freehealthier
Prep30 min
Cook75 min
Total105 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Allergens: none of the common ones

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 800g shoulder, in large cubes with some fat Pork
  • 12 Kashmiri, soaked Dried Red Chilli
  • 1/2 cup Vinegarpalm vinegar if you can find it
  • 15 cloves Garlic
  • 2 tbsp Ginger
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1 tsp Black Pepper
  • 2cm Cinnamon
  • 6 Cloves
  • 4 Cardamomoptional
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 1 tsp Jaggerybalances the vinegaroptional
  • 2, sliced Onion
  • 3 tbsp Neutral Oil
  • to taste Salt

Equipment

stovetop, blender

Before you start

  • Vindaloo is not simply 'very hot'. The name is vinha d'alhos — wine and garlic — and the defining flavours are sour and garlicky, with Kashmiri chillies for colour rather than fire.
  • Grind the masala with vinegar, never water. The vinegar is both the flavour and the preservative that let this dish travel.

Method

  1. Grind the soaked chillies, garlic, ginger and whole spices with the vinegar to a smooth, thick paste. Use no water.
  2. Rub half the paste into the pork with salt and marinate at least 4 hours, ideally overnight refrigerated.
  3. Fry the onion in oil until soft and golden, then add the remaining masala paste and cook until the oil separates.
  4. Add the pork with its marinade and turn it over high heat until sealed and coated.
  5. Add a cup of hot water, cover, and simmer 60 minutes until the pork is tender and the fat has rendered into the gravy.
  6. Balance with jaggery and salt. Rest overnight if you can — vindaloo is markedly better on day two.

Storage

Genuinely improves for 3-4 days refrigerated; the vinegar preserves it. Freezes 3 months.
