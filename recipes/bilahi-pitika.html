Northeast Indian

Bilahi Pitika

fire-roasted tomatoes mashed with raw mustard oil, onion and green chilli

vegetarianvegangluten freedairy freenut freehealthier
Prep10 min
Cook10 min
Total20 min
Serves4
Difficultyeasy

Allergens: none of the common ones

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 4 large (about 500g), ripe and soft Tomato
  • 2 tbsp, raw Mustard Oilunheated — the raw pungency is the whole point of a pitika
  • 1 small, very finely chopped Onion
  • 3, finely chopped Green Chillistanding in for bhut jolokia, which the catalogue has no id for; the local chilli is many times hotter, so use a fraction of one if you have it
  • 3 tbsp, chopped Coriander Leaves
  • to taste Salt
  • 1/2, juiced Lemononly if the tomatoes turn out sweet rather than souroptional

Equipment

stovetop

Before you start

  • Choose tomatoes that are properly ripe and a little soft. Firm supermarket tomatoes roast into something watery and bland.
  • Chop the onion, chilli and coriander before you start roasting, so everything goes into the warm pulp at once.
  • Mustard oil goes in raw here. If yours is very sharp, warm it to smoking point, cool it fully, and use it cold.

Method

  1. Set the tomatoes directly on a gas flame over medium heat and turn them with tongs every couple of minutes, 8-10 minutes in all, until the skins blister black and split and the flesh sags.A flame, not a pan — the smoky char is half the flavour. Under a hot grill for 15 minutes is the next best thing.
  2. Drop the hot tomatoes into a bowl and cover it for 5 minutes.The trapped steam loosens the skins so they slip off instead of tearing.
  3. Peel away and discard the skins, tipping every drop of juice back into the bowl.
  4. Mash with a fork or your fingers to a coarse, lumpy pulp.Stop while there are still recognisable pieces. Blended smooth it becomes a sauce, not a pitika.
  5. Fold in the raw mustard oil and salt and leave it 2 minutes.
  6. Stir in the onion, green chilli and coriander. Taste, and add the lemon juice only if it tastes flat.
  7. Serve at room temperature in a heap beside plain rice, with dal poured over the rice.

Storage

Best within a couple of hours. The mashed tomato keeps 2 days refrigerated, but the onion goes limp and grey — hold it back and stir fresh onion, chilli and coriander in just before serving.
