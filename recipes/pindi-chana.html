Punjabi

Pindi Chana

chickpeas blackened with tea and whole spice, with no tomato and no onion

dairy freeegg freegluten freehealthierno onion garlicnut freeveganvegetarian
Prep20 min
Cook60 min
Active80 min
Plus45 min soaking
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Allergens: none of the ten listed below

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Ingredients Method Nutrition

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 300g, soaked overnight Chickpeas
  • 1 tsp Black Pepper
  • 4 Dried Red Chilli
  • 1 stick Cinnamon
  • 2 Black Cardamomthe smoke in the dish
  • 4 Cloves
  • 2 Bay Leaf
  • 2 tsp Amchur
  • 1 tbsp, ground Anardanapomegranate seed; the sourness that makes it pindi
  • 2 tbsp, julienned, to finish Ginger
  • 4, slit, to finish Green Chilli
  • 4 tbsp Neutral Oil
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop, pressure cooker

Before you start

  • Named for Rawalpindi. It has no onion, no garlic and no tomato: the colour comes from cooking the chickpeas with the whole spices, traditionally in a muslin bag with tea leaves.
  • It should be almost dry and very dark, not a gravy.

Method

  1. Boil the soaked chickpeas with the cinnamon, black cardamom, cloves and bay leaves until soft and dark, about 45 minutes or 5 whistles.The whole spices go in the water, not the pan. That is where the colour comes from.
  2. Drain, keeping a cup of the cooking liquid. Fish out the whole spices.
  3. Grind the pepper, dried chillies and anardana together coarsely.
  4. Heat the oil, add the ground mixture and fry 1 minute, then the chickpeas.
  5. Toss on high 8 minutes, crushing a handful against the pan, adding splashes of the reserved liquid to keep it moving.
  6. Finish with the amchur, raw ginger julienne and slit green chillies.

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and improves overnight. Add the ginger fresh when serving.
