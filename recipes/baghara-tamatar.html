Hyderabadi

Baghara Tamatar

whole tomatoes simmered in the peanut, sesame and coconut gravy of the salan family

dairy freeegg freegluten freehealthierveganvegetarian
Prep20 min
Cook35 min
Total55 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Contains: peanuts, sesame, mustard

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Ingredients Method Nutrition

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 500g small firm tomatoes, left whole and pricked twice each Tomato
  • 60g, skin on Peanut
  • 3 tbsp Sesame
  • 3 tbsp, grated Dried Coconut
  • 1 large, sliced Onion
  • small lump, soaked in 100ml hot water Tamarind
  • 1 tbsp paste Ginger
  • 1 tbsp paste Garlic
  • 3, slit Green Chilli
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1/2 tsp Nigellaoptional
  • 1/4 tsp Fenugreek Seedsoptional
  • 2 sprigs Curry Leaves
  • 1.5 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 1 tsp Coriander Powder
  • 1 tsp Jaggeryoptional
  • 6 tbsp Neutral Oila salan gravy needs the oil; it separates out and is spooned off at the end
  • 2 tbsp, chopped Coriander Leavesoptional
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop, kadhai, blender

Before you start

  • Choose small, firm tomatoes and prick each one twice with a fork so the gravy gets inside without the skin bursting.
  • Soak the tamarind in hot water for 15 minutes and strain before you start cooking.
  • Roast the peanuts, sesame and coconut separately, not together. They brown at completely different speeds.

Method

  1. Dry-roast the peanuts over medium heat 5 minutes, until the skins blister. Cool and rub off most of the skins.
  2. Toast the sesame 2 minutes until it pops, then the coconut 1 minute until golden. Cool everything.
  3. Fry the sliced onion in 2 tbsp of the oil until golden, 10 minutes, then cool.
  4. Grind the peanuts, sesame, coconut and fried onion with 150ml water to a smooth, thick paste.Grind this longer than feels necessary. Gritty paste makes a gravy that never comes together.
  5. Heat the remaining oil in a kadhai and pop the mustard seeds, cumin, nigella and fenugreek, 20 seconds. Add the curry leaves and green chillies.
  6. Add the ginger and garlic pastes and fry 1 minute, then the whole tomatoes, and roll them in the oil for 3 minutes.
  7. Lift the tomatoes out and set aside. Add the ground paste with the chilli powder, turmeric and coriander powder, and fry over medium heat 8 minutes.The paste catches easily. Stir every 30 seconds and scrape the base; you are waiting for the oil to rise to the surface.
  8. Pour in the strained tamarind water and 250ml plain water, add the salt and jaggery, and bring to a simmer.
  9. Return the tomatoes, cover, and simmer on low 12 minutes, spooning gravy over them twice, until they are soft but still whole.Do not stir hard once the tomatoes are back in, or they collapse into the gravy.
  10. Uncover and simmer 5 minutes more, until a slick of red oil separates on top. Scatter the coriander and rest 10 minutes before serving with bagara rice.

Storage

Keeps 4 days refrigerated and improves overnight as the tomatoes take up the gravy. Reheat gently on the stovetop without stirring much; freeze up to 2 months.
