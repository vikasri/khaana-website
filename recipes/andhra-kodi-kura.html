Andhra

Andhra Kodi Kura

bone-in chicken curry, fiercely red and thick with browned onion

dairy freeegg freegluten freehealthier
Prep20 min
Cook45 min
Total65 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Contains: peanuts

Checked against the ingredient list for milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Nothing else is checked, and brands vary, so read the label on anything you have not bought before.

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 800g bone-in, cut into curry pieces Chickenbone-in only; boneless breast has nothing to give this gravy
  • 3 medium, finely sliced Onion
  • 2 medium, chopped Tomato
  • 3cm piece Gingerpounded with the garlic to a coarse paste
  • 10 cloves Garlic
  • 4, slit Green Chilli
  • 2.5 tsp Chilli PowderGuntur chilli powder; drop to 1.5 tsp if that is more heat than you want
  • 1.5 tbsp Coriander Powder
  • 3/4 tsp Turmeric
  • 1 tsp, coarsely crushed Black Pepper
  • 1 tsp Garam Masalaadded at the end
  • 4 Cloves
  • 2cm stick Cinnamon
  • 1 Bay Leafoptional
  • 1 tbsp Poppy Seedsground with 3 tbsp water to a smooth paste; thickens the gravy the Andhra wayoptional
  • 2 sprigs Curry Leaves
  • 5 tbsp Groundnut Oil
  • a handful, chopped Coriander Leavesoptional
  • 1.5 tsp, or to taste Salt
  • 400ml Water

Cookware

stovetop, kadhai

Before you start

  • Rub the chicken with 1/2 tsp turmeric, 1/2 tsp salt and 1 tsp of the chilli powder then cover and refrigerate it 30 minutes.
  • Pound the ginger and garlic together in a mortar rather than blitzing them. The coarse paste browns better and does not go watery.
  • Soak the poppy seeds in warm water for 20 minutes before grinding, or they stay gritty.

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a kadhai until it shimmers, add the cloves, cinnamon and bay leaf and let them swell for 30 seconds.
  2. Add the sliced onion with a pinch of salt and fry over medium heat for 14 to 16 minutes, until deep brown at the edges and shrunk to a third of the volume.This is the backbone of the curry and the one step you cannot shorten. Pale onion means a thin, sharp gravy.
  3. Stir in the ginger-garlic paste, green chillies and curry leaves and cook 3 minutes until the raw garlic smell has gone completely.
  4. Add the tomato and cook 6 minutes, pressing it against the pan, until it breaks down and the mixture pulls together.
  5. Add the chilli powder, coriander powder, remaining turmeric and crushed pepper and fry 2 minutes over low heat with a splash of water so the spices do not catch.Powdered spices burn in about 40 seconds on a dry hot pan. The splash of water is your insurance.
  6. Tip in the marinated chicken and turn it in the masala over high heat for 8 minutes, until every piece is coated and the edges have seized and browned.
  7. Stir in the poppy seed paste and the salt, then pour in 400ml water and bring to a boil.
  8. Lower the heat, cover, and simmer 20 minutes, until the chicken is tender and a thigh piece comes off the bone easily.
  9. Uncover and cook 8 minutes more on medium heat, until the gravy clings to the chicken and a slick of red oil rises to the top.The oil separating is when it is done. Serve it before that and it tastes unfinished.
  10. Sprinkle over the garam masala and coriander leaves, cover, and rest 5 minutes off the heat.

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and is better on day two. Freezes for 2 months. Reheat over low heat with 3 tbsp water, stirring gently so the chicken does not shred.
