Devilled Kidneys

lamb kidneys in a sharp, peppery sauce, eaten on toast at breakfast

egg freenut free
Ingredients Method Nutrition
Prep15 min
Cook12 min
Active27 min
Plus20 min soaking
Serves2
Difficultymoderate

Contains: milk, gluten

Ingredients

Quantities for 2.

  • 300g kidneys, cored and halved Muttonlamb kidney; soak 20 minutes in salted water and rinse
  • 40g Butter
  • 1, finely chopped Onion
  • 1.5 tsp, coarsely crushed Black Pepper
  • 1 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 1 tbsp Vinegar
  • 3 tbsp Creamoptional
  • 4 slices, toasted Bread
  • a handful Coriander Leavesoptional
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Before you start

  • Devilled means hot and sharp: pepper, mustard and something acid. It is a breakfast dish and a fast one.
  • Kidneys are ruined by overcooking. Six minutes total, and they should still be pink at the centre.

Method

  1. Soak the cored kidneys in salted water 20 minutes, then rinse and pat completely dry.Drying matters. Wet kidneys stew.
  2. Melt the butter and soften the onion 5 minutes.
  3. Raise the heat, add the kidneys and sear 2 minutes without moving them.
  4. Add the pepper and chilli powder, toss 2 minutes, then the vinegar and cream.
  5. Season, spoon over hot toast and scatter coriander.Serve the moment it is done. Held warm, kidneys go grey and tough.

Safe temperatures: Whole cuts of mutton, beef and pork are done at 63°C / 145°F, rested 3 minutes. A long braise passes that well before the meat is tender. Reheat leftovers to 74°C / 165°F. FoodSafety.gov

Storage

Eat immediately. This does not keep or reheat.
