Pahari

Babru

shimla-style fried breads packed with spiced ground urad dal, crisp outside and savoury through

vegetarianvegandairy freenut free
Prep25 min
Cook25 min
Total50 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Contains: gluten

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 300g Atta
  • 50g Maidaa little refined flour keeps the shell crisp rather than chewyoptional
  • 150g split skinned, soaked 4 hours Urad Dal
  • 600ml, for deep frying Neutral Oil
  • 1 tbsp, for the dough Mustard Oil
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1/2 tsp Ajwain
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetida
  • 2cm, grated Ginger
  • 2, finely chopped Green Chilli
  • 1 1/2 tsp Coriander Powder
  • 1 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 1 tsp Amchur
  • 1 tsp, coarsely crushed Fennel Seeds
  • 1/2 tsp Garam Masala
  • 2 tbsp, chopped Coriander Leavesoptional
  • to taste Salt
  • 180ml Water

Equipment

stovetop, kadhai

Before you start

  • Soak the urad dal 4 hours, then drain it hard in a sieve for 10 minutes. Wet dal makes a sloppy filling that tears the dough.
  • The dough needs 30 minutes rest before rolling, so make it first and the filling while it sits.
  • Grind the dal coarse, not smooth - babru filling should be grainy.

Method

  1. Mix the atta, maida, 1 tsp salt and the mustard oil, then add water gradually to a firm, smooth dough. Knead 6 minutes.
  2. Cover and rest 30 minutes. The dough should press back slowly when poked.
  3. Grind the drained urad dal with the green chillies and ginger to a coarse, dry paste, using at most 2 tbsp water.If it looks like batter you have gone too far. Stop while you can still see broken grains.
  4. Heat 2 tbsp of the frying oil in a pan, crackle the cumin, ajwain and asafoetida, then add the dal paste.
  5. Cook the dal on medium for 8 to 10 minutes, breaking up lumps, until it dries out and comes away from the pan in a single crumbly mass.Filling that is still moist will steam inside the babru and burst it open in the oil.
  6. Stir in the coriander powder, chilli powder, amchur, crushed fennel, garam masala, salt and coriander leaves. Cook 2 minutes, then cool completely.
  7. Divide the dough into 10 balls. Flatten each in your palm, cup it, put in a heaped tablespoon of cold filling, gather the edges over the top and pinch shut. Press flat.
  8. Roll each stuffed ball gently to about 10cm across, working from the centre out with light pressure.Press hard and the filling pushes through. If a tear opens, patch it with a scrap of dough.
  9. Heat the oil to 175C. Slide in two babrus at a time and press the centre lightly with a slotted spoon so they puff.
  10. Fry 2 minutes a side until deep golden and blistered. Drain and serve hot with kaddu ka khatta or a tamarind chutney.

Storage

Eat within a couple of hours while the shell is crisp. They keep a day in an airtight tin and revive in a 180C oven for 5 minutes; refrigeration makes them leathery.
