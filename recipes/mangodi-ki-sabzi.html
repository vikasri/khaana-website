Mangodi ki Sabzi

sun-dried moong dal nuggets fried crisp and stewed in a tart tomato masala

dairy freeegg freegluten freenut freeveganvegetarian
Ingredients Method Nutrition
Prep25 min
Cook30 min
Active55 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Contains: mustard

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 150g split moong dal, soaked and ground into nuggets Moong Dalor 120g ready-made mangodi from the shop
  • 5 tbsp Neutral Oil3 for frying the mangodi, 2 for the masala
  • 3 medium, pureed Tomato
  • 6 cloves, crushed Garlic
  • 1 tbsp, grated Ginger
  • 2, slit Green Chilli
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1/2 tsp Mustard Seedsoptional
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep this gluten-free
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 1 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 2 tsp Coriander Powder
  • 1 tsp Amchurthe sourness that makes this a desert dish
  • 1/2 tsp Garam Masala
  • 2 tbsp, chopped Coriander Leavesoptional
  • to taste Salt
  • 600ml Water

Cookware

stovetop, kadhai, blender

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Before you start

  • To make mangodi from scratch: soak the moong dal 4 hours, grind to a thick fluffy paste with almost no water, beat in a pinch of salt, then drop pea-sized blobs onto a cloth and dry them in full sun for 2-3 days until they rattle. They keep a year in a jar, which is the whole point of them.
  • If you are using shop-bought mangodi, sort out any broken crumbs first. They burn in the oil and turn the sabzi bitter.
  • Puree the tomatoes ahead; chopped tomato leaves lumps that never break down in the short cook.

Method

  1. Heat 3 tbsp oil in a kadhai over medium heat and fry the mangodi in two batches, stirring constantly, until they turn a deep reddish brown and smell nutty, about 3 minutes a batch. Drain on paper.They colour all at once at the end. Stand over them.
  2. Tip out all but 2 tbsp of oil. Crackle the cumin and mustard seeds, then add the hing and let it foam for a second.
  3. Add the garlic, ginger and green chilli and fry 90 seconds until the garlic is pale gold at the edges.
  4. Stir in the turmeric, chilli powder and coriander powder with 2 tbsp water so they do not scorch, then pour in the tomato puree.
  5. Cook the masala on medium heat 8-10 minutes, stirring, until it thickens, darkens and the oil pools at the sides.No oil at the sides means the tomato is still raw and the sabzi will taste sharp.
  6. Add 600ml water and salt, bring to a boil, then slide in the fried mangodi.
  7. Simmer uncovered 12-15 minutes until the mangodi have swollen to twice their size and are soft right through but still hold their shape.Bite one at 12 minutes. Overcooked mangodi dissolve into the gravy.
  8. Stir in the amchur and garam masala, simmer 2 minutes more, and finish with coriander. Serve with bajre ki roti.

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and thickens considerably; loosen with hot water and re-simmer 3 minutes. Uncooked mangodi keep a year in an airtight jar.
