Tamil Nadu

Adai

a thick, coarse-ground four-lentil crepe, nuttier and heavier than dosa

vegetarianvegangluten freedairy freenut freehealthier
Prep20 min
Cook25 min
Total45 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Allergens: none of the common ones

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 1 cup raw rice Riceidli rice or any short-grain raw rice
  • 1/3 cup Toor Dal
  • 1/3 cup Chana Dal
  • 1/3 cup Urad Dal
  • 6 Dried Red Chillibyadgi or guntur, to taste
  • 1/4 tsp Asafoetidause compounded-free hing to keep this gluten-free
  • 2 sprigs, torn Curry Leaves
  • 2.5cm piece, roughly chopped Ginger
  • 1 medium, finely chopped Onionstirred into the batter raw, never ground inoptional
  • 3 tbsp, fresh grated Coconutoptional
  • 2 tbsp, chopped Coriander Leavesoptional
  • 6 tbsp, for the griddle Sesame Oilgingelly oil is the Tamil default
  • about 200ml for grinding Water
  • to taste Salt

Cookware

stovetop, tawa, blender

Before you start

  • Soak the rice and all three dals together in plenty of water for 3 hours. Soak the dried chillies with them so they grind smooth.
  • Adai batter is never fermented. Grind it and cook within the hour, or refrigerate and use the same day.
  • Chop the onion and coriander only after grinding — they go in at the last moment or the batter turns watery.

Method

  1. Drain the soaked rice, dals and chillies, keeping the soaking water aside.Do not throw the soaking water away; you will want a little of it to loosen the grind.
  2. Grind everything with the ginger, curry leaves, asafoetida and salt, adding water in small splashes, to a coarse batter with the texture of wet semolina.Coarse is the point. A silky adai batter cooks into a limp pancake instead of a crisp one.
  3. Transfer to a bowl and stir in the chopped onion, coconut and coriander. The batter should be thick enough to mound on a spoon.
  4. Rest the batter 20 minutes while the tawa heats over medium.
  5. Pour a ladleful onto the hot tawa and spread outward with the back of the ladle to a 20cm round, about 5mm thick — thicker than a dosa.
  6. Make a coin-sized hole in the centre and drizzle 1 tbsp sesame oil around the rim and into the hole.The hole lets steam escape so the middle cooks through instead of staying pasty.
  7. Cook 3 to 4 minutes until the edges lift and the underside is a mottled deep brown.
  8. Flip and cook 2 minutes more, pressing lightly with the spatula, then serve hot with avial or plain jaggery and butter.

Storage

Adai is best straight off the tawa. The batter keeps 2 days refrigerated and in fact grows tastier on day two; bring to room temperature and thin with a spoon of water before cooking.
