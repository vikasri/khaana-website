Parsi

Jardaloo Ma Gosht

mutton braised with dried apricots until the gravy turns sweet, sour and dark

gluten freedairy freenut free
Prep20 min
Cook80 min
Total100 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Allergens: none of the common ones

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 700g bone-in shoulder, cut into 4cm pieces Mutton
  • 14 whole Dried ApricotHunza apricots with the stone in are the traditional choice
  • 3 medium, finely sliced Onion
  • 1 tbsp paste Ginger
  • 1 tbsp paste Garlic
  • 2 medium, chopped Tomato
  • 1 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 1 tsp Kashmiri Chillifor colour without more heat
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • 2 tsp Coriander Powder
  • 1 tsp Cumin Powder
  • 1 tsp Garam Masala
  • 1 piece, 3cm Cinnamon
  • 4 Cloves
  • 1.5 tbsp, grated Jaggery
  • 2 tbsp Vinegarcane vinegar if you have it
  • 2 medium, cut into fine matchsticks and fried crisp Potatothe sali toppingoptional
  • 4 tbsp Neutral Oil
  • to taste Salt
  • 500ml Water

Equipment

stovetop, pressure cooker

Before you start

  • Soak the apricots in 200ml warm water for 30 minutes. Keep the soaking water — it goes into the gravy.
  • Bring the mutton to room temperature and pat it dry, or it will steam rather than brown.
  • If you are making sali at home, soak the potato matchsticks in cold water 15 minutes, dry them thoroughly, then fry in batches.

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a heavy pan and fry the cinnamon and cloves for 30 seconds, until the cloves swell.
  2. Add the sliced onion and cook over medium heat until deep brown, 18-20 minutes, stirring so the edges do not catch.This is where the colour of the dish comes from. Brown, never black — burnt onion turns the whole gravy bitter.
  3. Stir in the ginger and garlic paste and fry 2 minutes, until the raw smell goes.
  4. Add turmeric, coriander, cumin, chilli powder and Kashmiri chilli. Fry 40 seconds with a splash of water so they do not scorch.
  5. Add the mutton and brown it hard for 8 minutes, until the pieces are sealed and the oil separates at the edges.
  6. Add the tomato and salt, cover, and cook 10 minutes until the tomato collapses into the masala.
  7. Pour in the water, bring to a boil, then simmer covered 55-65 minutes until the mutton is tender. In a pressure cooker, 4 whistles then 10 minutes on low.
  8. Add the soaked apricots with their water, the jaggery and the vinegar. Simmer uncovered 12 minutes.Add the apricots this late or they dissolve. You want them whole and plump against the meat.
  9. Taste and balance: it should read sweet first, then sour, then hot. Adjust with a little more jaggery or vinegar.Balancing is the whole recipe. Do it off the heat, a teaspoon at a time.
  10. Stir in the garam masala, rest 5 minutes off the heat, and pile the fried sali on top just before serving.Sali goes on at the table. Sat in the gravy it goes soft in two minutes.

Storage

Keeps 3 days refrigerated and tastes better on day two. Reheat gently with a splash of water; fry fresh sali rather than reheating old.
