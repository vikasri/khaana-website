Andhra

Bobbatlu

thin griddled flatbreads stuffed with sweet chana dal and jaggery

egg freenut freevegetarian
Prep30 min
Cook40 min
Active70 min
Plus3 hr resting
Serves4
Difficultyadvanced

Contains: milk, gluten

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Ingredients Method Nutrition

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 200g, rinsed Chana Dal
  • 200g, grated Jaggerydark jaggery gives the proper colour and depth
  • 200g Maida
  • 50g Attaa little wholewheat keeps the dough manageable; all maida is the traditional choiceoptional
  • 1/8 tsp Turmericfor colour in the dough onlyoptional
  • 6 pods, seeds powdered Cardamom
  • a pinch, grated Nutmegoptional
  • 6 tbsp, melted, plus more for the griddle Ghee
  • 1/4 tsp Salt
  • about 120ml Water

Cookware

stovetop, tawa, pressure cooker, blender

Before you start

  • Make the dough at least 3 hours ahead. An underrested bobbattu dough will not stretch and will tear every time.
  • Soak the chana dal 30 minutes before cooking so it softens evenly.
  • Cook the filling and cool it completely before stuffing; warm filling turns the dough sticky and unworkable.

Method

  1. Knead the maida, atta, salt, turmeric and about 120ml water into a very soft, sticky dough. Softer than chapati dough, almost batter-like at the edges.
  2. Pour 4 tbsp of the melted ghee over the dough, cover, and leave it 3 hours at room temperature. It should be slack and silky when you come back.The dough must be soft enough to spread with your fingers. If it holds a firm ball shape, work in another tablespoon of water.
  3. Pressure cook the drained chana dal with 400ml water for 4 whistles, until a grain crushes to paste between two fingers.Cook it soft but drain it hard. Wet dal makes filling that will not hold together.
  4. Drain the dal thoroughly, then blitz or mash it to a smooth paste.
  5. Cook the dal paste with the jaggery in a heavy pan over medium heat for 12 to 15 minutes, stirring constantly, until it thickens and comes away from the sides in a single mass.It will loosen and go watery when the jaggery melts. Keep going. It stiffens again, and that is when it is ready.
  6. Stir in the cardamom and nutmeg, spread the filling on a plate and cool it completely, then roll it into 8 equal balls.
  7. Oil your palms, take a lump of dough the size of a lime, flatten it on a greased sheet of banana leaf or baking paper, place a filling ball in the centre and gather the dough over it.
  8. With greased fingertips, pat the parcel out to a thin 15cm round. Do not use a rolling pin, it will tear.Work from the centre outwards in small pats. If the filling shows through in one spot, patch it with a scrap of dough.
  9. Heat a tawa over medium heat and flip the bobbattu onto it, paper side up, then peel the paper away.
  10. Cook 60 to 90 seconds a side, brushing with ghee, until brown spots appear and the surface puffs in patches.Medium heat only. High heat browns the outside before the filling warms through.
  11. Slide it onto a plate, brush with more ghee, and repeat with the rest.

Storage

Keeps 3 days at room temperature in an airtight box, or 8 days refrigerated. Warm on a dry tawa for 30 seconds a side before eating; microwaving makes them chewy.
