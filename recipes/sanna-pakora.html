Sindhi

Sanna Pakora

thin, shatteringly crisp gram flour fritters shot through with sliced onion

dairy freeegg freegluten freenut freeveganvegetarian
Prep20 min
Cook25 min
Active45 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Allergens: none of the ten listed below

Checked against the ingredient list for ten allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Brands vary, so read the label on anything new to you.

Ingredients Method Nutrition

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 1.5 cups Besan
  • 2 medium, sliced paper thin Onionsanna means fine. The thinner the slice, the crisper the pakora
  • 1 medium, sliced into 2mm rounds then halved Potato
  • 3, finely chopped Green Chilli
  • 1/3 cup, chopped Coriander Leaves
  • 1 tsp Ajwain
  • 1 tsp, coarsely crushed Anardanaor 1 tsp amchur
  • 1 tsp, coarsely crushed Coriander Powder
  • 1 tsp Chilli Powder
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric
  • a pinch Baking Sodafor lift, not moreoptional
  • 500ml for deep frying, plus 1 tbsp hot for the batter Neutral Oil
  • to taste Salt
  • about 1/3 cup Water

Cookware

stovetop, kadhai

Before you start

  • Salt the sliced onion and let it sit 10 minutes. It releases water that becomes most of your batter liquid, which is why the batter is so thick.
  • Have the oil heating while you mix, and the batter mixed only just before frying. It slackens and goes limp if it stands.
  • Set a rack, not paper, for draining. Paper traps steam under the pakoras and softens them.

Method

  1. Toss the sliced onion with 1 tsp salt in a wide bowl and leave 10 minutes, then squeeze it lightly over the bowl and keep the liquid.
  2. Add the potato, green chilli, coriander leaves, ajwain, crushed anardana, crushed coriander powder, chilli powder and turmeric to the onion and mix.
  3. Sprinkle the besan and baking soda over the top and rub everything together with your fingers for 2 minutes without adding water.The vegetables should be coated in dry besan first. Adding water too early makes a smooth batter that fries heavy.
  4. Add water a tablespoon at a time until the mixture just clumps in your hand. Thick and shaggy, nothing pourable.
  5. Heat the frying oil in a kadhai to 170C. A pinch of batter should rise to the surface in about 4 seconds.
  6. Stir 1 tbsp of the hot oil into the batter. It lightens the crumb.
  7. Drop rough, loose spoonfuls into the oil, 6 or 7 at a time, and fry 4 minutes until pale gold. Lift and drain on a rack.Do not press them into neat balls. Ragged edges are what turn crisp.
  8. Rest the first batch 5 minutes while you fry the rest.
  9. Return all the pakoras to the oil, now raised to 185C, and fry 90 seconds more until deep gold and hard to the touch.This double fry is the difference between a crisp pakora and a soggy one twenty minutes later.
  10. Drain and serve immediately with tamarind or tomato chutney.

Storage

Eat within the hour. Leftovers keep 1 day in an airtight box and can be crisped in a 200C oven for 6 minutes; never microwave them.
