Appam

Lacy fermented rice pancakes with a soft, thick centre

dairy freeegg freegluten freenut freeveganvegetarian
Prep20 min
Cook25 min
Total45 min
Serves4
Difficultymoderate

Allergens: none of the ten listed below

Checked against the ingredient list for milk, egg, fish, crustaceans, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, mustard, soy and gluten. Nothing else is checked, and brands vary, so read the label on anything you have not bought before.

Ingredients

Quantities for 4.

  • 2 cups, soaked 4 hours Rice
  • 1/2 cup grated Coconut
  • 1/2 cup Coconut Milk
  • 1/4 cup cooked Ricehelps the batter ferment and spreadoptional
  • 1 tsp Sugarfeeds the ferment
  • to taste Salt
  • for the pan Coconut Oil

Cookware

stovetop, blender

Before you start

  • Appam batter ferments like dosa batter but is thinner and sweeter. It should pour, not spread.
  • The pan matters: a deep, round-bottomed appachatti is what makes the edges lacy and the centre thick. A small wok is the closest substitute.

Method

  1. Grind the soaked rice with the grated coconut, cooked rice and enough water to make a smooth, pourable batter.
  2. Add sugar, cover, and leave to ferment 8-10 hours until risen and faintly sour.
  3. Stir in the coconut milk and salt. The batter should be thinner than dosa batter, like single cream.Too thick and you get a pancake; too thin and it will not hold a centre.
  4. Heat the appachatti, wipe with coconut oil, pour a ladle into the middle, then immediately lift the pan and swirl it once so batter climbs the sides.
  5. Cover and cook 2-3 minutes on low. Do not flip.The lid is what steams the thick centre while the edges crisp.
  6. Lift out when the edges lift away on their own. Serve with stew, kadala curry or sweetened coconut milk.

Storage

Batter keeps 3 days refrigerated and grows more sour; older batter suits appam less well than dosa. Cooked appams do not keep.
